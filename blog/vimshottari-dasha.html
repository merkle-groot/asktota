what a dasha is, and why ur life has chapters

a fixed 120-year cycle, split between nine planets, starting from wherever ur moon was standing at birth. here's how vimshottari dasha actually works, and why it's the timeline Vedic astrology runs on.

AUGUST 8, 2026 · 8 MIN READ

a birth chart tells u where the planets were. it doesn't, by itself, tell u when anything happens. dasha is the part of Vedic astrology that adds the timeline.

the system most Vedic astrology runs on is called vimshottari dasha, and once u see the mechanism, it stops being mysterious. it's a fixed 120-year cycle, divided into nine unequal chunks, one per planet, and where u start in that cycle is set entirely by your moon's position at birth.

the 120-year cycle

vimshottari means "one hundred twenty" in sanskrit, and that's exactly the length of the full cycle: 120 years, divided among nine grahas, each getting a fixed, unequal number of years. the split doesn't change from chart to chart. it's the same 120 years, the same nine shares, every time.

why it starts from a nakshatra, not a sign

dasha timing runs on a finer division of the zodiac than the twelve rashis: the 27 nakshatras, each covering 13°20' of the zodiac. each nakshatra is ruled by one of the nine dasha planets, and the 27 nakshatras cycle through all nine rulers exactly three times, which is why the nine-planet order repeats three times across the full zodiac.

your dasha sequence starts with whichever planet rules the nakshatra your moon was in at the moment u were born. that's the entire starting rule: find the moon's nakshatra, find its ruling planet, that planet's mahadasha is where your timeline begins.

why your first mahadasha is already partway through

u weren't born at the exact start of your first nakshatra, u were born somewhere inside it, and the dasha calculation accounts for that. the moon's position within the nakshatra, as a proportion, roughly how far through that 13°20' segment it had traveled, tells the system how much of that first planet's mahadasha had already "elapsed" before u were even born.

someone born early in a nakshatra starts their life with most of that first mahadasha still ahead of them. someone born late in the same nakshatra might only have a year or two of it left before moving into the next planet's period. same starting planet, very different timeline, because the moon's exact degree at birth is what does the math.

mahadasha and antardasha

a mahadasha isn't one flat block of years. it subdivides into antardashas, sub-periods where each of the nine planets gets a proportional slice of that mahadasha's total length, in the same fixed nine-planet order, starting from the mahadasha lord itself.

so "Saturn mahadasha, Mercury antardasha" means: you're in your roughly 19-year Saturn period overall, and currently inside the specific slice of it that belongs to Mercury. Saturn sets the broad theme for those 19 years; Mercury's antardasha colors the specific months u're in right now. antardashas subdivide further into pratyantardashas, and traditionally further still, but mahadasha and antardasha are the two levels that carry the most weight in a typical reading.

what a dasha is actually describing

each planet has a general character: the sun relates to identity and authority, the moon to mind and emotional life, Jupiter to growth and expansion, Saturn to structure and restriction, and so on. a mahadasha is read as that planet's general themes becoming more active in your life for the length of its period, filtered through where that planet sits in your specific chart: which house it occupies, which houses it rules, and what it's aspected by.

this is also why the same mahadasha plays out differently for different people. a Saturn mahadasha for someone with a strong, well-placed Saturn tends to read as disciplined, structured growth. the same mahadasha for someone with a weaker or more challenged Saturn tends to read as restriction that has to be worked through first. same nine-planet clock, different chart underneath it.

the receipt

the 120-year total, the nine unequal shares, the fixed sequence, and the nakshatra-based starting point are the standard classical definition of vimshottari dasha, the most widely used dasha system in Vedic astrology. it isn't the only dasha system in the tradition, but it's the default, and it's what "your dasha" almost always means unless someone specifies otherwise.

faq

what is vimshottari dasha?

a fixed 120-year planetary period system used in Vedic astrology to time events in a chart, divided among nine planets in unequal, fixed shares, starting from the ruling planet of your moon's nakshatra at birth.

why does dasha start from the moon and not the sun or ascendant?

vimshottari dasha specifically anchors to the moon's nakshatra, because the moon represents the mind, and dasha timing is traditionally read as the unfolding of mental and life experience over time.

how long does each dasha last?

it depends on the planet: Ketu and Mars get 7 years, the Sun gets 6, the Moon gets 10, Venus gets 20, Rahu gets 18, Jupiter gets 16, Saturn gets 19, and Mercury gets 17. all nine add up to 120 years.

what's the difference between mahadasha and antardasha?

a mahadasha is a planet's main period, one of the nine large slices of the 120-year cycle. an antardasha is a smaller sub-period within it, where each of the nine planets gets a proportional slice inside that mahadasha.

can two people have the same dasha sequence?

the nine-planet order is always the same, but where in that sequence a person starts, and exactly how long each period lasts for them, depends entirely on their moon's nakshatra and degree at birth. two people rarely start at the same point.

nine planets, one fixed 120-year cycle, and a starting point set by exactly where your moon was standing the day u were born. that's the whole mechanism behind why Vedic astrology talks about your life in chapters.
