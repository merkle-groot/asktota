what a rudraksha is

A rudraksha is the dried stone of the fruit of Elaeocarpus ganitrus, a broadleaf tree growing in the Himalayan foothills, Nepal and Southeast Asia. Its surface has natural vertical clefts called mukhis, usually between one and fourteen. In Shaiva tradition the beads are associated with Shiva and are worn for devotion and protection.

the legend behind the name is that the trees grew from shiva's tears, which is why "aksha" is read as tear as well as eye. the beads have been used for japa, repeating mantras on a mala, for many centuries.

the mukhis, one to fourteen

Each mukhi count is traditionally linked to a deity, and in modern astrology to a planet. A common mapping links one to nine mukhis to the Sun, Moon, Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, Saturn, Rahu and Ketu. Higher mukhis are linked to deities rather than single planets. Traditions vary in the details.

how to choose one

Most people start with a five mukhi, which is traditionally safe for anyone. If you want a bead for a specific planet, astrologers usually match it to a weak but important planet in your chart, such as your ascendant lord or current dasha lord. Unlike gemstones, a mismatched rudraksha is not considered harmful.

  • no specific reason? five mukhi.
  • planet based? match the mukhi to ur lagna lord or current dasha lord.
  • for a mala? smaller indonesian beads are lighter and more comfortable.
  • rare mukhi? insist on a lab certificate with an x-ray.

how to wear it

Traditionally a rudraksha is strung on red or yellow thread or a silver or gold chain, worn so it touches the skin, and energised with water and a Shiva mantra such as Om Namah Shivaya, often on a Monday. Many people remove it while sleeping or at funerals by custom.

care is simple: keep it clean, oil it occasionally with a little mustard or sandalwood oil to stop it drying and cracking, and avoid harsh soaps. a good bead lasts decades.

how fakes work, and how to check

Common fakes include beads with extra mukhis carved in, two beads glued together, beads made from other seeds or wood, and plastic or resin copies. The reliable verification is an X-ray or CT scan, which shows one internal seed compartment per genuine mukhi. Popular water and coin tests are not reliable.

the water test, "a real rudraksha sinks", is a myth: dense fakes sink too and real dry beads can float. the "spins between copper coins" test is a magnet trick. neither tells u anything.

rudraksha against gemstones

Gemstones are traditionally said to amplify a planet's influence, so classical practice prescribes them cautiously after chart analysis. Rudraksha is treated as devotional and protective, with very few traditional cautions, which is why it is widely worn without a chart. Neither has scientific evidence of an astrological or health effect.

myths, one at a time

A real rudraksha always sinks in water.

Density varies. Real beads can float and dense fakes sink. Only an X-ray is reliable.

One mukhi rudraksha is easy to buy.

Round one mukhi beads are extremely rare. Most on sale are other varieties or fakes.

The wrong rudraksha can harm you.

Tradition treats rudraksha as safe in a way it does not treat gemstones, especially five mukhi.

Rudraksha has proven health benefits.

There is no solid scientific evidence. Its value is devotional and personal.

faq

what is a rudraksha?

the dried seed of the rudraksha tree, elaeocarpus ganitrus, which grows in the himalayan foothills, nepal and southeast asia, especially indonesia. the name joins rudra, a name of shiva, and aksha, eye or tear.

what does mukhi mean in rudraksha?

mukhi means face. it refers to the natural vertical lines or clefts running from top to bottom of the bead. a five mukhi bead has five lines. the count is what determines the bead's traditional meaning.

which rudraksha is best for everyone?

the five mukhi, linked to shiva as kalagni rudra and to jupiter in the common planetary mapping. it is by far the most common, inexpensive, and traditionally considered safe for anyone to wear.

which rudraksha is for which planet?

the common modern mapping is: 1 mukhi sun, 2 moon, 3 mars, 4 mercury, 5 jupiter, 6 venus, 7 saturn, 8 rahu, 9 ketu. traditions differ slightly, especially above nine.

is one mukhi rudraksha real?

round one mukhi beads from nepal are extremely rare. most beads sold as one mukhi are half moon shaped varieties from south india or elsewhere, or are carved fakes. be very cautious of any one mukhi offered at a modest price.

how do i check if a rudraksha is real?

the reliable method is an x-ray or ct scan from a reputable lab, which shows internal compartments matching the number of mukhis. the popular water floating test and the copper coin spinning test are not reliable.

can anyone wear rudraksha?

traditionally yes, regardless of gender, age or religion. unlike gemstones, rudraksha is generally not considered to have harmful side effects, which is part of why five mukhi is so widely worn.

how should i wear a rudraksha?

traditionally on a red or yellow thread or a silver or gold chain, touching the skin, after a simple energising with water and a shiva mantra such as om namah shivaya. monday is a traditional day to start.

what is the difference between nepali and indonesian rudraksha?

nepali beads are larger, with deeper, clearer mukhis. indonesian beads are smaller and smoother, often used in malas. both are the same genus. price differences are mostly about size, clarity and rarity.

is rudraksha better than gemstones?

they work on different logics. gemstones are said to amplify a planet, so the tradition prescribes them carefully. rudraksha is treated as devotional and protective with few cautions. neither has scientific evidence of effect.

how many beads are in a rudraksha mala?

108 plus one, the guru or sumeru bead. 108 is the traditional number for japa, mantra repetition, and also 27 nakshatras times 4 padas.

does rudraksha have scientific benefits?

there is no solid scientific evidence for health or astrological effects. many people value rudraksha as a devotional object and a reminder of practice, which is a perfectly good reason to wear one.
