two families get the kundlis matched. the score comes back at 16 out of 36, and everything stops.

almost always, the reason is one item on the list. nadi. it is worth 8 points on its own, it is all or nothing, and when it fails it takes an eighth of the entire score with it.

here is what nadi actually is, why roughly a third of all couples fail it, and the exceptions that the same tradition wrote down centuries ago and almost nobody mentions.

the short version

  • nadi is one of the eight kootas in guna milan, and the heaviest. it carries 8 of the 36 points.
  • there are three nadis: aadi, madhya and antya. all 27 nakshatras are split evenly between them, nine each.
  • if both people's birth nakshatras fall in the same nadi, u score zero out of 8. that is nadi dosha.
  • three groups, evenly split, means roughly one couple in three hits this. it is not rare and it was never meant to be.
  • the classical texts already include exceptions: same rashi with different nakshatras, same nakshatra with different rashis, or different padas.
  • it is not used everywhere. much of south india uses a ten koota system that weights things differently.

what nadi actually is

Nadi is a three way grouping of the 27 nakshatras, borrowed from ayurvedic vocabulary. Each nakshatra belongs to aadi, madhya or antya. In kundli matching, two people whose birth nakshatras fall in the same group score zero on this koota, and that is called nadi dosha.

the word nadi means a channel or a pulse. the three names map onto the three ayurvedic doshas: aadi to vata, madhya to pitta, antya to kapha.

the underlying idea is constitutional. the tradition treats a shared nadi as two people made of the same stuff, and reads that as too much of one thing rather than a balance. the specific worry recorded in the texts is about health and children.

that is the reasoning, stated plainly. it is an analogy from ayurveda applied to birth stars. it is not a claim anyone has tested, and it should not be repeated as though it were medical advice.

mechanically, nadi comes from one number only: which nakshatra the moon was in when u were born. no houses, no lagna, no other planet. if u know ur birth nakshatra, u already know ur nadi.

which nadi u are, all 27 listed

Nine nakshatras belong to each nadi. Aadi: Ashwini, Ardra, Punarvasu, Uttara Phalguni, Hasta, Jyeshtha, Mula, Shatabhisha, Purva Bhadrapada. Madhya: Bharani, Mrigashira, Pushya, Purva Phalguni, Chitra, Anuradha, Purva Ashadha, Dhanishta, Uttara Bhadrapada. Antya: Krittika, Rohini, Ashlesha, Magha, Swati, Vishakha, Uttara Ashadha, Shravana, Revati.

the pattern hiding in the list

The nadi assignment is not random. It runs in groups of three, snaking back and forth: aadi, madhya, antya, then antya, madhya, aadi, then aadi, madhya, antya again, all the way round.

look at the first six. ashwini, bharani, krittika go aadi, madhya, antya. then rohini, mrigashira, ardra go antya, madhya, aadi. it has turned around and come back.

then it turns again for the next three, and keeps zigzagging across all 27. it is the same shape as an ox ploughing a field, and it is why the nine nakshatras in each group look scattered when u list them by name and look perfectly regular when u list them by number.

this is a nice thing to know for two reasons. it means the assignment is a designed structure rather than an arbitrary list, so u can reconstruct it instead of memorising it. and it means the middle position, madhya, always falls on the same slot in every group of three, which is why madhya nakshatras are every third one on the dot: 2, 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26.

tota says

before anyone panics about a nadi dosha, ask for the two rashis and the two nakshatra padas. the classical exceptions live in exactly those numbers, and they are part of the same tradition that gave u the score. u are not arguing with the system, u are reading the rest of it.

why it is worth eight points

Nadi carries the highest weight of the eight kootas because the tradition ties it to health and children rather than to temperament. It is also binary, so a couple either gets all 8 or none, with nothing in between.

why a third of couples fail it

There are three nadis and the 27 nakshatras split evenly, nine to each. So two people picked at random have about a one in three chance of landing in the same group. Nadi dosha is not a rare affliction, it is the expected outcome for roughly a third of all pairs.

the arithmetic takes a second. whatever nadi the first person is, the second person has a one in three chance of matching it. that is it. about 33 percent.

this is the single most useful fact in this article, because of how the dosha gets described. the language around it is the language of misfortune: affliction, defect, blocked. that language is doing work that the numbers do not support.

a condition that turns up in one couple in three is a normal outcome of a three way split. it is not a curse landing on the unlucky. it is what happens when u sort everyone into three bins and then check whether two people landed in the same one.

and it explains the remedy economy neatly. a defect affecting a third of all couples, in a country with millions of marriages a year, is an enormous and permanently renewing market.

the exceptions the texts already wrote

Classical matching practice includes cancellations for nadi dosha, usually given as three: the couple share a rashi but have different nakshatras, they share a nakshatra but have different rashis, or they share both and sit in different padas of that nakshatra.

these are called nadi dosha parihara, and they are part of the same tradition doing the scoring. they are not a loophole someone invented to be nice.

be honest about one thing here: different traditions and different families apply these exceptions differently, and there is no single authority everyone defers to. what u can do is ask which exception is being considered and why, which is a fair question and a revealing one.

the padas point is worth spelling out. every nakshatra is divided into four padas of 3 degrees 20 minutes each, and those padas are the same grid as the navamsa chart. so "different padas" is a real, calculable distinction, not a technicality.

the birth time problem

Nadi is derived from the moon's nakshatra, and the moon covers one nakshatra in a bit over 24 hours. A birth time that is several hours off can move someone into the next nakshatra, and therefore into a different nadi, which flips 8 points of the score.

the moon travels roughly 13 degrees a day. a nakshatra is 13 degrees 20 minutes wide. so the moon takes about a day and a quarter to cross one.

that sounds forgiving until u remember where the risk sits: at the edges. if someone was born with the moon near the end of a nakshatra, a few hours of error moves them to the next one, and one in three of those moves changes their nadi group.

which means a match that failed on nadi and a match that passed on nadi can be the same two people with one birth certificate corrected. worth checking before anyone makes a decision they cannot take back. how accurate ur birth time needs to be covers the general problem.

where nadi is not weighted this way

The eight koota system with nadi at 8 points is most associated with north indian practice. Much of south india uses a ten koota framework with different items and weights, and many communities do not treat nadi as decisive at all.

this is one of the most useful things to know when a nadi dosha comes up, because it reframes the whole conversation. u are not looking at a universal law of the tradition. u are looking at one regional scoring convention among several.

the 18 out of 36 threshold is the same kind of thing: a widely repeated convention rather than a number handed down by a single authoritative text. it became standard because it is half, and half is a natural place to draw a line.

none of that makes the system worthless. it makes it a tradition, with regional variants and reasonable disagreements inside it, which is exactly what an honest description of it should say.

what to actually do with a nadi dosha

work through it in this order, and most of the panic resolves itself.

  1. confirm both birth times. hospital records, not memory. this is the single most common source of a wrong nadi.
  2. get both nakshatras and both padas written down. and both rashis.
  3. check the three exceptions against those numbers. they are part of the tradition, and one of them applies more often than people expect.
  4. look at the other seven kootas. a score of 16 with a strong graha maitri and gana reads very differently from a 16 that is weak everywhere.
  5. decide as two people. the score is one traditional input with a known one in three failure rate. it is not a verdict on ur life together.

and if someone moves straight from the diagnosis to a price, that is the moment to slow down. the exceptions cost nothing and were written down long before the payment link existed.

myths, one at a time

Nadi dosha is rare and means we are badly matched.

Three nadis, evenly split, means about one couple in three. It is the expected outcome of the way the groups are drawn.

It will cause health problems for our children.

That is the traditional rationale, borrowed from ayurvedic ideas about constitution. There is no medical evidence linking birth star groups to fertility or child health.

Only an expensive remedy can clear it.

The classical exceptions require no payment. Check those first, because they come from the same tradition as the score.

A score below 18 means the marriage will fail.

18 is a widely used convention, not a law. And with nadi worth 8 on its own, a single failed koota can drag an otherwise strong match under the line.

Nadi matching is used across all of india.

The 8 koota system is one regional convention. Much of south india uses a 10 koota framework with different weights.

Nadi dosha is the same as manglik dosha.

Completely different things. Manglik is about mars in certain houses of one person's chart. Nadi is a comparison between two people's moon nakshatras.

faq

what is nadi dosha?

it is what happens when both people in a match have birth nakshatras belonging to the same nadi group. nadi is one of the eight kootas in guna milan, it carries 8 of the 36 points, and it scores zero when the two match.

what are the three nadis?

aadi (vata), madhya (pitta) and antya (kapha). the names come from ayurveda. all 27 nakshatras are assigned to one of the three, nine to each.

how common is nadi dosha?

about one couple in three. there are three nadis and a roughly even split of nakshatras between them, so the chance of two people landing in the same group is close to one in three.

why is nadi worth 8 points?

it is the highest weighted koota in the ashtakoota system, traditionally justified by a claim about health and children. because it is all or nothing, losing it drops a score by 8 and often takes a couple under the usual 18 point line.

what are the exceptions to nadi dosha?

the classical exceptions are usually given as: the two share a rashi but have different nakshatras, they share a nakshatra but have different rashis, or they share both but sit in different padas of that nakshatra.

does nadi dosha affect children?

the traditional claim is about progeny and health, borrowed from ayurvedic ideas about constitution. there is no medical evidence connecting birth star groupings to fertility or child health.

can nadi dosha be cancelled by a puja?

there is a remedy industry around it, and there are also textual exceptions that require no payment at all. check the exceptions first, because they are part of the same tradition doing the scoring.

is nadi dosha used everywhere in india?

no. the eight koota system is most associated with north indian practice. much of south india uses a ten koota system with different weights and a different emphasis, and many communities do not weight nadi this heavily.

does birth time change my nadi?

it can. nadi comes from ur moon's nakshatra, and the moon spends a bit over a day in each one. a birth time wrong by several hours can move u to the neighbouring nakshatra and therefore to a different nadi.

should a couple call off a wedding over nadi dosha?

that is a decision about two people, and it should be made by those two people. the score is one traditional metric with a known one in three failure rate, and the tradition itself wrote down exceptions for exactly this case.

how do i find my nadi?

find ur moon's nakshatra at birth, then look up which of the three groups it belongs to. nakshatras 1, 6, 7, 12, 13, 18, 19, 24 and 25 are aadi. 2, 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23 and 26 are madhya. the rest are antya.

what is the minimum guna score for marriage?

18 out of 36 is the commonly quoted floor, with 24 and above treated as good. those thresholds are convention rather than a rule from a single authoritative text.
