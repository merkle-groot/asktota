transits are counted from ur moon, not ur lagna

almost every transit post u have read counted from the wrong place. classical gochar is measured from ur moon sign. here are the favourable houses for every graha, the cancellation rule nobody mentions, and the number that keeps transits in proportion.

OCTOBER 26, 2026 · 12 MIN READ

almost every transit post u have ever read counted from the wrong place.

classical gochar is measured from ur moon sign. not ur ascendant, not ur sun sign. that is the tradition's own convention, it changes every answer, and it is left out of nearly all modern transit content.

here is the whole system: what is favourable where, the cancellation rule almost nobody mentions, and the one number that keeps all of it in proportion.

the short version

  • gochar is transit: where the grahas are now, read against ur birth chart.
  • it is counted from ur moon sign. that is the classical convention and it is the thing most transit posts get wrong.
  • each graha has its own list of favourable houses counted from there. saturn's are 3, 6 and 11.
  • vedha cancels a result when another graha sits in a specific counterpart house. it is core to the system and almost always omitted.
  • ashtakavarga bindus are how a transit gets personalised beyond ur moon sign cohort.
  • a transit result applies to about a twelfth of everyone alive at once. hold that number next to any transit claim.

what gochar actually is

Gochar is where the grahas are in the sky right now, read against your birth chart. It describes conditions, not schedule. In Vedic practice the schedule is the dasha, and a transit is generally read as triggering something the dasha has already made available.

hold those two apart, because collapsing them is the commonest mistake in this whole area.

that third row is why transit content spreads so easily. a claim about ur moon sign cohort can be written once and aimed at a hundred million people. a claim about ur dasha cannot, because it needs ur birth details.

why it is counted from the moon

Classical gochar counts the transiting graha's position from the natal Moon sign. So if your Moon is in Karka and Saturn is transiting Tula, Saturn is in your 4th from the Moon, and that is what the tradition reads.

two reasons, and both are good ones.

one: gochar is about the mind's response. the moon carries manas, and a transit system asking "how does this land" is naturally anchored to the graha that does the landing. the moon is also the graha vedic astrology weights hardest generally, so this is consistent rather than arbitrary.

two: it works without a birth time. the moon sign needs only a date and a rough time. the lagna needs the minute. in a tradition where exact birth times were frequently unavailable, a transit system that runs on the moon is a system that runs for everybody.

many practitioners now read from both the moon and the lagna and weight what appears in both. that is a reasonable modern refinement. what is not reasonable is reading from the lagna alone while calling it classical gochar, which is what most transit content does without saying so.

next time u read "saturn transits ur 7th house", ask from where. if the answer is ur sun sign, that is western astrology in sanskrit clothing. if it is ur lagna, it is a modern reading. classical gochar counts from the moon, and the three give three different houses.

read down the good column and a pattern jumps out. 3, 6 and 11 appear for every malefic. those are the upachaya houses, where harsh grahas are said to improve with use, and the transit system is simply applying the same principle the natal system does. we went through upachaya houses here.

venus is the outlier with nine favourable houses out of twelve, which is about what u would expect from the second great benefic.

and note saturn's row. the 12th, 1st and 2nd from the moon is sade sati, and the 4th and 8th is shani dhaiya. those are not separate doctrines. they are the difficult part of saturn's ordinary gochar, given names.

vedha: the cancellation nobody mentions

Vedha means obstruction. Classical gochar pairs each favourable house with a counterpart house, and if another graha occupies that counterpart at the same time, the favourable result is cancelled. The same applies in reverse to difficult results.

this is a core part of the system and it is missing from almost every popular transit reading, which is a problem, because it means those readings are quoting half a rule.

the mechanism is simple enough to state. a transit result is not read in isolation. it is read together with what is sitting in its vedha house at that moment. an occupied vedha point neutralises the reading.

two consequences worth carrying:

  • a good transit can deliver nothing. if its vedha point is occupied, the tradition says the result does not arrive.
  • a bad transit can also deliver nothing, for the same reason. this is the half that gets omitted most often, because it is the half that would reduce fear.

there is a documented exception: the sun and saturn are said not to obstruct each other, since they are father and son in the puranic account. the tradition is at least consistent about its own stories.

the practical test: if someone gives u a transit result and cannot say what its vedha point is or whether it is occupied, they are using a simplified version and should say so.

ashtakavarga, and how a transit gets personal

Ashtakavarga scores every sign from each graha's perspective, awarding points called bindus out of a maximum of eight. A transit through a sign with a high bindu count is read as easier than the same transit through a low-scoring one.

this is the part of the system that answers the obvious objection to all transit reading: everyone with my moon sign has this transit, so how can it mean anything specific?

ashtakavarga is the answer, and it is a good one. the bindu count for each sign is derived from ur own chart, so two people with the same moon sign can have very different scores in the sign saturn is currently crossing.

bindus in the transited signhow the transit is read
0 to 3difficult. the sign is weak for that graha in ur chart
4neutral
5 to 8easier. the graha has support there

so the honest ordering is: the gochar table gives a cohort-level reading, vedha can cancel it, and ashtakavarga adjusts it to ur specific chart. any transit claim that skips the last two is running at a twelfth-of-the-planet resolution.

the double transit

A house is treated as genuinely activated when Jupiter and Saturn both influence it at the same time, by sitting in it or aspecting it. Because their speeds differ so much, this is uncommon and does not last long, which is exactly why it is weighted.

jupiter takes about a year per sign, saturn about two and a half. for both to reach the same house at once takes a specific alignment, and it passes.

the reading is intuitive once u know what each graha does. jupiter expands and saturn consolidates. one supplies enthusiasm, the other supplies weight. together they are said to produce something that actually happens and then stays happened.

it is the most cited rule in marriage timing for exactly this reason, applied to the 7th house from the lagna and from the moon.

and it is a rare example of a transit rule with a built-in scarcity. a claim that only applies during an uncommon, short window is a claim with something at stake, which is more than most transit content manages.

only the slow ones matter

The moon changes sign every 2.25 days and mercury moves constantly. Those are day-to-day weather. The transits that describe a chapter of a life are saturn, jupiter, and the rahu ketu axis.

this is also the honest answer to the mercury retrograde question. a three week transit of the fastest personal graha is not the instrument this tradition reaches for when it wants to say something about a life. we ran that argument in full here.

the twelfth-of-the-planet problem

Gochar is counted from the Moon sign, and there are twelve of those. So any transit result applies to roughly one in twelve people alive, all at the same time. Roughly 700 million people share your Saturn transit reading right now.

this is the number that keeps everything else in proportion, and it is not an argument against transits. it is an argument about resolution.

what gochar can honestly say: this is the general weather for people with ur moon sign, this quarter. that is a real statement and it is what the classical system is built to produce.

what it cannot honestly say: what happens to u specifically. that requires the layers above it, and even then the tradition puts the dasha first.

so the working order, and it is the same order a practitioner uses: chart, then dasha, then transit, then vedha, then ashtakavarga. anything that starts at transit and stops there is describing a twelfth of humanity and charging u for it.

faq

what is gochar?

the sanskrit term for transit: where the grahas are in the sky right now, read against ur birth chart. it is the background weather of a chart, as opposed to the dasha, which is the schedule.

are transits read from the lagna or the moon?

classical gochar is counted from the moon sign. that is the tradition's own convention and it is the single most common thing modern transit content gets wrong.

why the moon and not the ascendant?

gochar is a system about the mind's response to conditions, and the moon carries the mind. it also means the reading works without a birth time, which matters in a tradition where exact times were often unavailable.

which houses are favourable for each graha?

counted from the moon: the sun is favourable in 3, 6, 10 and 11. the moon in 1, 3, 6, 7, 10 and 11. mars in 3, 6 and 11. mercury in 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 and 11. jupiter in 2, 5, 7, 9 and 11. venus in 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 8, 9, 11 and 12. saturn in 3, 6 and 11.

what is vedha in gochar?

an obstruction. if another graha occupies a specific counterpart house at the same time, the transit result is cancelled. it is a core part of the classical system and it is left out of nearly every popular transit reading.

what is the double transit rule?

a house is treated as genuinely activated when jupiter and saturn both influence it at the same time, by sitting in it or aspecting it. because their speeds differ so much, that is uncommon and short, which is why it carries weight.

what is ashtakavarga and how does it refine transits?

a points system giving each sign a score from each graha's perspective, out of eight. a transit through a sign with a high bindu count is read as easier than the same transit through a low-scoring sign, which personalises a result that would otherwise apply to everybody.

how many people share a transit result with me?

roughly a twelfth of everyone alive, because gochar is counted from the moon sign and there are twelve of those. that is the number that keeps any transit claim in proportion.

which transits actually matter?

the slow ones. saturn at about 2.5 years per sign, jupiter at about a year, and the rahu ketu axis at about 18 months. the moon changes sign every 2.25 days and mercury moves constantly, so those are day-to-day weather rather than chapters.

is a transit more important than a dasha?

no, and the tradition is clear about this. dashas are the timing engine and run in chunks of years. transits describe conditions. a transit is generally read as triggering something the dasha has already made available.

does a bad transit mean something bad will happen?

it describes a condition that applies to a twelfth of the population simultaneously. what it produces in one life depends on the chart underneath it, the dasha running, and a great many things astrology never claimed to see.

what is the difference between gochar and the natal chart?

the natal chart is fixed at birth and never changes. gochar is where the grahas are today. one is who u are, the other is what the weather is doing, and confusing them is the commonest error in transit content.
