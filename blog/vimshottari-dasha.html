what a dasha is, and why ur life has chapters

a fixed 120-year cycle, split between nine planets, starting from wherever ur moon was standing when u were born. here is how vimshottari dasha works, and why it is the clock vedic astrology runs on.

AUGUST 8, 2026 · 8 MIN READ

a birth chart is a photo. it shows where the planets were standing. on its own it never tells u when anything happens.

dasha is the part that adds time. the version nearly everyone uses is called vimshottari dasha, and once u see how it is built it stops feeling mysterious.

picture a 120-year clock face, cut into nine slices of different sizes, one slice per planet. everybody gets the same clock. the only thing that changes from person to person is which slice u start on. and that is decided by where ur moon was standing the moment u were born.

the 120-year cycle

vimshottari is sanskrit for "one hundred twenty", and that is exactly how long the full cycle runs. 120 years, shared out between nine grahas, each one getting a set number of years.

the shares are not equal, and they never change. same 120 years, same nine slices, every chart, every time.

each planet's turn is called its mahadasha, which just means "great period". the order in the table, Ketu down to Mercury, never changes. it is always that loop, and it starts over at the top.

why it starts from a nakshatra, not a sign

the twelve rashis are big. dasha needs something finer, so it uses the 27 nakshatras instead. each nakshatra is a slice of sky 13°20' wide, and each one is owned by one of the nine dasha planets.

nine planets into 27 nakshatras goes exactly three times. so the nine-planet order simply repeats three times around the sky.

the starting rule is one sentence. find the nakshatra ur moon was in when u were born, find the planet that owns it, and that planet's mahadasha is where ur timeline opens. that is it.

why your first mahadasha is already partway through

u were almost certainly not born at the exact edge of a nakshatra. u were born somewhere in the middle of one, and the maths cares about that.

think of the nakshatra as a song already playing. if ur moon was a quarter of the way through it, then a quarter of that first mahadasha was already spent before u arrived. u join partway through.

so someone born near the start of a nakshatra gets almost the whole first period. someone born near the end of the very same nakshatra might get a year of it and then move straight on to the next planet. same starting planet, completely different timeline, decided by ur moon's exact degree.

mahadasha and antardasha

a mahadasha is not one flat block of years. it splits again.

inside it, all nine planets get a turn, each one taking a share in the same fixed order, starting with the mahadasha's own planet. these smaller turns are called antardashas.

so "Saturn mahadasha, Mercury antardasha" means ur inside ur roughly 19-year Saturn stretch, and right now ur in the Mercury part of it. Saturn sets the mood for the whole 19 years. Mercury colours the months ur living through today.

it keeps splitting after that, into pratyantardashas and finer still, but these top two levels carry most of the weight in a normal reading.

what a dasha is actually describing

each planet has a character. the sun is about who u are and who is in charge. the moon is about ur mind and feelings. Jupiter is about growing. Saturn is about rules and limits.

a mahadasha is read as that planet's themes turning up the volume for the length of its turn. but it gets filtered through ur own chart: which house that planet sits in, which houses it rules, and which planets are looking at it.

that is why the same mahadasha feels different for different people. a Saturn period for someone with a strong Saturn usually reads as hard work that pays off. the same period for someone with a struggling Saturn reads more like a wall u have to get over first. same clock, different chart underneath it.

the receipt

the 120-year total, the nine uneven shares, the fixed order and the nakshatra starting point are the standard classical definition of vimshottari dasha. it is the most used dasha system in vedic astrology.

it is not the only one in the tradition. but it is the default, and when someone says "ur dasha" without explaining, this is what they mean.

faq

what is vimshottari dasha?

a fixed 120-year cycle used in vedic astrology to work out when things happen. it is split between nine planets in set, uneven shares, and u start on whichever planet owns the nakshatra ur moon was in at birth.

why does dasha start from the moon and not the sun or ascendant?

because in vedic astrology the moon stands for the mind, and dasha is read as ur inner life unfolding over time. so the clock is set from the moon's nakshatra.

how long does each dasha last?

it depends on the planet. Ketu and Mars get 7 years each. the Sun gets 6, the Moon 10, Venus 20, Rahu 18, Jupiter 16, Saturn 19 and Mercury 17. the nine add up to 120.

what's the difference between mahadasha and antardasha?

a mahadasha is one of the nine big slices of the 120-year cycle. an antardasha is a smaller slice inside it, where all nine planets each get a short turn.

can two people have the same dasha sequence?

the nine-planet order is always the same. but where u start, and how much of the first period is left when u arrive, comes from ur moon's exact nakshatra and degree. so two people rarely line up.

nine planets. one fixed 120-year cycle. a starting point set by exactly where ur moon was standing the day u were born. that is the whole reason vedic astrology talks about ur life in chapters.
