ashtakavarga is the part that makes a transit actually urs

OCTOBER 6, 2026 · 11 MIN READ

the standard objection to transits is that they apply to a twelfth of the planet at once. it is a good objection.

it is also one the tradition raised first, and answered with a points system. ashtakavarga scores every sign in ur chart out of eight, from eight different points of view, and the scores are different for everybody. it is the layer that turns "saturn is in ur 7th" into a number that belongs to u and to nobody with a different chart.

here is how the points are counted, what the totals mean, and the one number that tells u whether a transit is landing on solid ground or on nothing.

the short version

  • ashtakavarga means eight divisions. eight reference points each vote on every sign.
  • a vote is a bindu. each sign ends up with 0 to 8 bindus from each graha's point of view.
  • bhinnashtakavarga is one graha's chart of scores. sarvashtakavarga is all seven added together.
  • the sarva total across all twelve signs is always 337, so the average sign scores about 28.
  • a transit through a high scoring sign is read as easier than the same transit through a low scoring one.
  • it needs ur whole chart, including the lagna, which means it needs a birth time.
  • it is the tradition's own answer to "this reading applies to everyone born in the same month".

quick facts

Sanskrit
Ashtakavarga, from ashta (eight) and varga (division).
Who votes
The seven grahas plus the Lagna. Rahu and Ketu are not included in the classical system.
A bindu
One benefic point given by one contributor to one sign.
Per sign range
0 to 8 in a single graha's chart. 0 to 56 when all seven are summed.
Sarva total
Always 337 across the twelve signs, in every chart ever cast.
Average sign
337 divided by 12, so about 28.
Sources

Mainly used for
Judging transits, and ranking the strength of houses.

eight voters, twelve signs

Ashtakavarga asks eight reference points in your chart, the seven grahas and the Lagna, to score each of the twelve signs. Each one gives a sign either a point or nothing, according to fixed classical rules about which houses it counts from itself.

the rule is mechanical and that is the appeal. the sun, for example, contributes a point to the 1st, 2nd, 4th, 7th, 8th, 9th, 10th and 11th houses counted from itself, and a different list counted from each of the other contributors. every graha has its own list. no interpretation is involved anywhere in the counting.

so u end up with a grid: eight voters, twelve signs, a yes or a no in every cell. everything else in ashtakavarga is a way of adding that grid up.

rahu and ketu are not in the voting. some modern practitioners add them, most classical texts do not, and mixing the two conventions is how people end up comparing scores that were never on the same scale. the nodes do plenty elsewhere in the chart.

what a bindu actually is

A bindu is a single benefic point. In one graha's chart each sign scores from 0 to 8, because there are eight possible contributors. High means that graha has support in that sign. Low means it is working with nothing.

bindus in a signhow it is read
0 to 3weak. the graha is unsupported there
4neutral. the result depends on everything else
5 to 8strong. the graha has backing in that sign

bindu means dot, and that is how it is drawn: a dot in a box. the older name for the same thing is rekha, a line. the terminology varies by region and the arithmetic does not.

the important thing about a bindu count is that it is specific to ur chart. two people with the same moon sign will have different bindu counts in the same sign, because the counting is done from the positions of every graha in their own charts. that is the entire point of the system.

tota says

ashtakavarga is the part of jyotish that behaves like arithmetic. nobody interprets a bindu into existence. the counting rules are fixed, two competent astrologers get the same grid, and any disagreement is about what the grid means rather than what is in it.

the totals, and why they never change

Each graha's own chart adds up to the same total in every chart: Jupiter always has 56 bindus spread across the twelve signs, Saturn always 39, and so on. Only the distribution changes.

this is a genuinely useful constraint. the total is fixed, so a chart cannot be good everywhere. a sign scoring well means another sign is scoring badly, in ur chart, by arithmetic. astrology is not often this honest about trade-offs.

notice which grahas get the most points to hand out. jupiter has 56 and saturn has 39, and that ordering matches how the tradition talks about the two of them everywhere else. the lagna has its own chart totalling 49, and it is left out of the seven graha sum.

sarvashtakavarga, the one number to read first

Sarvashtakavarga is the sum of all seven grahas' bindus in each sign. It ranges from 0 to 56 per sign, the twelve signs always total 337, and the average sign scores about 28. It is the single fastest read of which parts of a chart are carrying weight.

sarva score in a signhow it is read
30 and abovea strong house. things done here tend to hold
25 to 29around average. ordinary
below 25thin. this area takes more effort for the same result

read it as a shape rather than a league table. the two or three highest signs are where the chart is dense, and they are worth knowing before any transit is discussed at all. a practitioner who opens with the sarva line is telling u which houses in ur chart have something to defend.

this is also the number that makes the houses concrete. the 10th house is career in every chart. whether ur 10th has 34 bindus or 19 is not the same in every chart, and it is the difference between a career house that supports effort and one that absorbs it.

using it on a transit

Find the sign a graha is transiting, then look up how many bindus that graha has in that sign in your own chart. Saturn crossing a sign where your Saturn bindus are 6 is a different transit from Saturn crossing a sign where they are 1, even though both are the same transit in the sky.

this is the whole reason the system survives. classical gochar gives u a cohort reading, and ashtakavarga is the layer that makes it yours.

stepwhat it tells u
1find the house from ur moon signthe classical gochar reading. shared with a twelfth of everyone
2check for vedhawhether the result is cancelled outright
3look up that graha's bindus in the signwhether the transit has support in ur chart
4check the sarva score of the same signwhether that area of the chart is dense or thin generally

the classic worked example is sade sati. two people can be in sade sati at the same time, in the same signs, and one of them has five or six saturn bindus in those signs while the other has one. the sky is identical. the reading is not, and the difference is arithmetic rather than opinion.

kakshya, the finer cut

Each sign divides into eight kakshyas of 3 degrees 45 minutes, one for each contributor, in a fixed order: Saturn, Jupiter, Mars, Sun, Venus, Mercury, Moon, Lagna. A transiting graha is read as supported while it crosses a kakshya belonging to a contributor that gave that sign a bindu.

this is where ashtakavarga stops being a sign level tool and starts producing dates. a graha moving through a sign passes through eight kakshyas in order, and the bindu pattern says which of those stretches have support behind them and which do not.

for a slow graha the stretches are long. saturn takes around two and a half years to cross a sign, so a single kakshya is roughly three or four months of it. that is a real window, and it is narrow enough to be wrong about, which is more than most transit content risks.

there are further classical refinements above this, the reduction techniques known as trikona and ekadhipatya shodhana. practitioners disagree about when to apply them and some skip them entirely. that disagreement is old, it is documented, and anyone presenting ashtakavarga as one settled procedure is smoothing over it.

what it cannot do

Ashtakavarga measures support, not outcome. A high bindu count says an area of your chart has backing; it does not say what will happen there, and it does not replace the dasha, which is what the tradition uses for timing.

three honest limits, since the arithmetic makes it tempting to over-read.

it needs a birth time. the lagna is one of the eight contributors, and the house positions depend on it. a chart cast with the wrong lagna produces a clean, confident, wrong grid. this is the one place where an approximate birth time quietly ruins a calculation rather than loudly failing.

it ranks, it does not predict. a 34 in the 10th house means the career house has support. it does not name a job, a date or an amount, and any reading that gets from a bindu count to a salary figure did that on its own.

the dasha still comes first. vimshottari is the timing engine. ashtakavarga tells u how well supported a thing is when its time comes. the order is chart, then dasha, then transit, then this.

myths, one at a time

Ashtakavarga is a modern invention.

It appears in Brihat Parashara Hora Shastra, Phaladeepika, Prashna Marga and Sarvartha Chintamani. It is one of the older computational parts of the tradition.

A high score means good things will happen.

It means that sign has support. Support is not outcome, and the total is fixed at 337, so a chart cannot score well everywhere.

You can get your ashtakavarga from your date of birth.

No. The Lagna is one of the eight contributors and the houses depend on it, so it needs a birth time as well as a date.

Rahu and Ketu have bindus too.

Not in the classical system. Some modern practitioners add them. Mixing the conventions produces totals that are not comparable to anybody else's.

A low sarva score in a house is a problem to be fixed.

It is a description of where the chart is thin. Every chart has thin signs, because the twelve always add to the same 337.

Ashtakavarga replaces the dasha.

It refines transits. The dasha remains the timing system, and a transit is generally read as triggering what a dasha has already made available.

faq

what is ashtakavarga?

a points system that scores each sign in ur chart from eight reference points: the seven grahas and the lagna. each one either gives a sign a point or does not, according to fixed classical rules.

what is a bindu?

one benefic point. in a single graha's chart each sign scores 0 to 8, because there are eight possible contributors. 5 or more is read as supported, 3 or fewer as thin.

what is the difference between bhinnashtakavarga and sarvashtakavarga?

bhinnashtakavarga is one graha's own table of scores across the twelve signs. sarvashtakavarga is all seven of those tables added together, so each sign gets a single number from 0 to 56.

why does sarvashtakavarga always total 337?

because each graha's own total is fixed: sun 48, moon 49, mars 39, mercury 54, jupiter 56, venus 52 and saturn 39. those add to 337 in every chart, so only the distribution across the signs changes.

what is a good sarvashtakavarga score?

the average sign scores about 28, since 337 divided by twelve is 28.1. above 30 is read as a strong house and below 25 as a thin one.

how does ashtakavarga improve a transit reading?

it makes it personal. classical gochar gives u a reading shared with everyone who has ur moon sign. the bindu count for that graha in that sign is specific to ur chart, so the same transit reads differently for two people.

do i need my birth time for ashtakavarga?

yes. the lagna is one of the eight contributors and the house positions depend on it. without a birth time the grid can still be calculated and it will quietly be the wrong grid.

are rahu and ketu included?

not in the classical system. the eight contributors are the seven grahas and the lagna. some modern practitioners add the nodes, which produces totals that no longer match the classical ones.

what is kakshya in ashtakavarga?

each sign divided into eight parts of 3 degrees 45 minutes, one per contributor, in the order saturn, jupiter, mars, sun, venus, mercury, moon, lagna. a transiting graha is read as supported while crossing a kakshya whose owner gave that sign a bindu.

is ashtakavarga used for sade sati?

routinely. two people in sade sati in the same signs can have very different saturn bindu counts in those signs, and that difference is the standard explanation for why one person's sade sati is harder than another's.

what are the reduction techniques?

trikona shodhana and ekadhipatya shodhana, two classical procedures for adjusting the totals before reading them. practitioners disagree on when to apply them, which is a real and well documented disagreement rather than a gap in the sources.

does a low bindu count mean something bad will happen?

no. it means that graha has little support in that sign in ur chart, so results there tend to take more effort. every chart has low scoring signs, by arithmetic.
