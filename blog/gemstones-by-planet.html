gemstones by planet, and when not to wear one

nine grahas, nine stones, and an industry built on top of them. here is the complete navaratna table, the two schools that flatly disagree about who should wear what, and the questions that keep u from being robbed.

SEPTEMBER 7, 2026 · 11 MIN READ

the reading ends, and there is a stone at the end of it. usually pukhraj, sometimes neelam, always in a shop the astrologer happens to know.

the gemstone tradition is genuinely old and genuinely detailed. nine grahas, nine stones, specific metals, specific days, specific weights. it is a real system with real internal logic.

it is also, right now, one of the largest and least regulated markets in indian astrology. so here is both halves: the complete tradition, and the questions that stop u being robbed inside it.

the short version

  • the navaratna are the nine planetary gems: ruby, pearl, red coral, emerald, yellow sapphire, diamond, blue sapphire, hessonite and cat's eye.
  • the tradition prescribes a metal, a day, a finger and a weight for each, and the details vary between practitioners.
  • two schools flatly disagree. one says strengthen ur weakest graha. the other says never strengthen a graha that rules difficult houses for ur lagna. that is not a small difference.
  • there is no evidence that a mineral affects a planet or an outcome. the tradition is real. the causal claim is not established.
  • upratna, the substitute stones, are part of the tradition and cost a fraction of the price. they are almost never offered first.
  • if u buy, separate the advice from the sale, demand an independent lab certificate, and refuse urgency.

the nine stones, in one table

Ruby for the sun, pearl for the moon, red coral for mars, emerald for mercury, yellow sapphire for jupiter, diamond for venus, blue sapphire for saturn, hessonite for rahu and cat's eye for ketu. Each comes with a traditional metal, weekday and finger.

the fingers and metals vary between practitioners more than any published table admits. if two astrologers give u different fingers, neither of them is lying to u. the tradition genuinely has regional variants.

weights are prescribed in ratti, a traditional unit of about 0.91 carat. this is worth knowing because shops price in carats, and a stone quoted as "five ratti" is about 4.5 carats. the slippage between the two units is one of the quieter places money goes missing.

the disagreement at the heart of it

Two schools give opposite advice. One says wear the stone of ur weakest or most afflicted graha, to strengthen it. The other says never strengthen a graha that rules difficult houses for ur lagna, and only ever wear stones for ur lagna lord and ur yogakaraka.

this is the single most important thing on this page, and almost nobody selling stones will raise it with u.

take a concrete case. mesha lagna, weak mercury. the strengthening school says wear an emerald. the functional school points out that for a mesha lagna, mercury rules the 3rd and the 6th, so an emerald would be strengthening the lord of ur house of conflict and debt, and refuses.

same chart, same graha, opposite advice, both defensible inside the tradition. so the useful question to any astrologer prescribing a stone is not "which stone." it is "which school are u following, and why does it apply to my lagna." a real practitioner has an answer ready. a shop does not.

tota says

never buy the stone from the person who told u to buy the stone. that one rule removes most of the fraud in this entire market, and it costs u nothing except a slightly awkward conversation.

why ur lagna decides everything

A graha is not universally good or bad. What it rules depends on ur ascendant, so the same planet is helpful for one lagna and difficult for another. Any gemstone advice given without ur lagna is advice given without the information it needs.

saturn is the cleanest example. for a vrishabha or tula lagna, saturn rules a kendra and a trikona and is a yogakaraka, the most helpful graha in the chart. for a mesha or simha lagna it rules difficult houses and is treated very differently.

so "should i wear neelam" has no answer until someone knows ur lagna. and yet blue sapphire is routinely sold to people who were never asked for their birth time at all.

if u only take one test away from this page, make it that one. if nobody asked for ur birth time, nobody calculated ur lagna, and no honest gemstone advice is possible without it. ur lagna matters more than ur sun sign covers why.

the blue sapphire problem

Blue sapphire is saturn's stone, the most expensive of the nine in most markets, and the one surrounded by the most dramatic claims. It is typically sold with a three day trial, a test structured so that any outcome confirms the seller was right.

the trial usually goes like this: wear it for three days, and if something good happens, the stone suits u. if something bad happens, take it off immediately, it does not suit u.

look carefully at what that offers. over three days, something good and something bad will both happen to almost anyone. so the test cannot fail. it produces a confirmation either way, which means it is not a test at all.

that is not an accusation of dishonesty against everyone who repeats it. it is a description of the logic. a claim that no possible outcome could contradict is not telling u anything about the stone.

and the money involved is real. neelam and panna are the two stones where price per carat climbs fastest with size and clarity, which makes them the natural upsell. if a reading ends with saturn, fear and a specific shop, slow all the way down. saturn in ur chart, minus the fear is the version of that conversation with no stone attached.

upratna: the cheaper half of the tradition

Upratna are substitute stones, traditionally accepted in place of the main gem for each graha. They are part of the classical practice, they cost a small fraction of the primary stone, and they are rarely offered first.

the existence of this table is itself informative. a tradition that offers a recognised cheaper option for every single stone is not a tradition that believes only the expensive version works. if the substitutes are acceptable, then the price was never the mechanism.

stones that are not worn together

Traditional practice avoids wearing stones for grahas treated as enemies at the same time. The most commonly cited pairs to avoid are blue sapphire with ruby or red coral, and diamond with ruby or pearl.

the reasoning follows the classical friendship tables. saturn's enemies include the sun, the moon and mars. venus and the sun are not friends. so the stones inherit those relationships.

the navaratna ring, which sets all nine stones together, sits oddly against this rule, and different practitioners resolve that differently. some treat the combined ring as a balanced whole. others avoid it for exactly this reason.

this is a good moment to notice something honest about the whole system: there is no single authority. multiple internally consistent versions coexist, and they disagree. that is normal for a living tradition and it should be said out loud rather than papered over.

how not to get robbed

The gemstone market is largely unregulated. The protections that actually work are structural: separate the advice from the sale, require independent certification, get treatment disclosed in writing, and refuse any urgency.

  1. separate the advice from the sale. if the person reading ur chart also sells the stone, or sends u to one specific shop, they have a financial interest in the diagnosis. that does not make them dishonest, it makes the advice unverifiable.
  2. demand an independent lab certificate. from a laboratory u chose, not one whose report arrived with the stone. it should state species, natural or synthetic, origin where relevant, weight in carats, and treatments.
  3. get treatment status in writing. heated, fracture filled, glass filled, dyed. treated stones are legitimate products at much lower prices. the problem is only ever an undisclosed treatment.
  4. check price per carat, not the total. quote the same specification to two or three unrelated sellers before u buy anything.
  5. refuse urgency. "it must be worn before the next amavasya" is a closing technique. no legitimate reading collapses if u take a week.
  6. be suspicious of anyone who did not ask ur birth time. without a lagna there is no functional analysis, so there is no basis for the recommendation.
  7. set a budget before the reading. a number decided while u are calm is a very different number from one decided while u are frightened.

what the evidence actually says

There is no known mechanism by which a mineral worn on the hand affects a planet or a life outcome, and no replicated study showing such an effect. The gemstone tradition should be described as a tradition, and the difference between a tradition and a treatment should not be blurred.

that is the honest position, and it does not require anyone to be contemptuous about it.

things people find in these practices that are real: a sense of agency during a difficult period, a daily reminder of an intention, a connection to family practice, an object that marks a decision. those are genuine and they are not nothing.

things that are not established: that the stone changes what a planet does, that it alters outcomes, or that a specific weight in ratti produces a specific effect.

where it stops being harmless is money and medicine. a stone bought at a price that hurts, or worn instead of seeing a doctor, has crossed from tradition into damage. the tradition itself never asked for either of those.

myths, one at a time

Everyone should wear their birth stone.

Half the tradition says the opposite: only wear stones for grahas that rule well for ur lagna. Prescribing without a lagna is prescribing without the data.

A more expensive stone works better.

The tradition itself lists an accepted cheaper substitute for every single one of the nine, which is difficult to square with price being the mechanism.

If it does not suit you, you will know in three days.

Over three days something good and something bad happen to nearly everyone. A test that confirms the seller whatever happens is not a test.

Blue sapphire either makes you rich overnight or ruins you.

This is the most dramatic claim attached to the most expensive stone, which is worth noticing on its own.

A gemstone can replace a remedy, a doctor or a decision.

No tradition claims this. It is added by sellers, and it is the point where the practice starts doing real harm.

Synthetic and treated stones are frauds.

They are legitimate products at legitimate prices. The fraud is selling one as the other. That is why disclosure in writing matters more than the treatment itself.

faq

which gemstone is for which planet?

ruby for the sun, pearl for the moon, red coral for mars, emerald for mercury, yellow sapphire for jupiter, diamond for venus, blue sapphire for saturn, hessonite for rahu and cat's eye for ketu. together they are the navaratna, the nine gems.

how do i know which gemstone i should wear?

there is no single answer, because two schools disagree. one says strengthen ur weakest graha. the other says never strengthen a graha that rules difficult houses for ur lagna, and only ever wear stones for ur lagna lord and ur yogakaraka. ask which school ur astrologer follows.

is there evidence gemstones work?

no. there is no measured mechanism by which a mineral affects a planet or a life outcome, and no replicated study showing an effect. the tradition is real and old. the causal claim is not established.

which gemstones should never be worn together?

traditional practice avoids pairing stones of grahas treated as enemies, most commonly blue sapphire with ruby or with red coral, and diamond with ruby or pearl. practitioners differ on the details.

what is a ratti?

the traditional indian weight unit for gemstones. one ratti is roughly 0.91 carat, or about 0.18 grams. prescriptions are usually given in ratti, while the market prices in carats, and the gap between the two is a common place to be overcharged.

what are upratna or substitute stones?

cheaper stones traditionally accepted in place of the main gem. red garnet for ruby, moonstone for pearl, carnelian for coral, peridot or green tourmaline for emerald, citrine or yellow topaz for yellow sapphire, white sapphire for diamond, amethyst or iolite for blue sapphire.

why is blue sapphire considered risky?

because it is saturn's stone and saturn is the graha of consequence, tradition treats it as the most reactive of the nine. that reputation is also why it is sold with a three day trial, a test framed so that any outcome confirms the seller's advice.

which finger should i wear my gemstone on?

commonly given as: ruby and yellow sapphire on the index or ring finger, pearl on the little finger, coral on the ring finger, emerald on the little finger, diamond on the middle or ring finger, blue sapphire, hessonite and cat's eye on the middle finger. practitioners vary.

does the gemstone need to touch my skin?

traditional practice says yes, the stone should be set so its underside touches the skin. that is a convention of the practice, not a physical requirement of anything.

how do i avoid being sold a fake?

buy the stone and the advice from different people, insist on a certificate from an independent laboratory, get the treatment status in writing, check price per carat against the open market, and never accept urgency.

is a heated or treated sapphire acceptable?

traditional practice generally prefers untreated natural stones, and treated ones cost far less. the important thing is that u are told which u are buying, in writing, before u pay.

what should i do instead of buying a stone?

if the goal is to feel less anxious about a chart, read the chart properly first. most gemstone purchases follow a frightening reading, and a clear reading removes the fear more reliably than a mineral does.
