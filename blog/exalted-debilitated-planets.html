every exaltation sits exactly opposite its debilitation

same degree, opposite sign, every single time. here is the full table, the four signs that have neither, the reason mercury breaks the pattern, and the cancellation rules that stop a debilitation being a verdict.

OCTOBER 14, 2026 · 10 MIN READ

somebody tells u a planet is debilitated and it lands like a diagnosis.

here is the first thing worth knowing: roughly half of all charts contain at least one debilitated graha. the second is that every exaltation sits exactly opposite its debilitation, at the same degree, in a pattern so tidy it is clearly designed rather than observed.

this is the reference page the rest of the chart desk keeps pointing at. full table, the four signs that have neither, and the cancellations.

the short version

  • uchcha is exalted, neecha is debilitated. each is a sign, with a specific peak degree.
  • they are always exactly opposite, at the same degree. every graha, no exceptions.
  • four signs have neither: mithuna, simha, dhanu and kumbha.
  • mercury is the only graha exalted in a sign it owns, both in kanya.
  • about half of all charts contain at least one debilitated graha. it is not rare and it is not a verdict.
  • neecha bhanga cancels a debilitation, and some texts then read it as a strength.

the full table

Each graha has one exaltation sign with a peak degree, and one debilitation sign directly opposite at the same degree. Here is the complete set for the seven visible grahas, plus the commonly used but disputed positions for the nodes.

the pattern hiding in it

Read the exaltation column against the rulership column and the design shows: a graha is usually exalted in a sign belonging to a graha whose nature supplies what it lacks.

  • the sun in mesha, mars's sign of unilateral action. the self in the sign that does not ask permission.
  • mars in makara, saturn's sign of structure. force with a plan behind it.
  • jupiter in karka, the moon's sign of nurture. expansion in the sign that gives without accounting.
  • venus in meena, jupiter's sign of dissolved boundaries. love where the edges between people are softest.
  • saturn in tula, venus's sign of weighing. consequence applied fairly.

and every debilitation is the mirror. saturn in mesha is patience in the sign of impatience. mars in karka is confrontation in the sign that avoids it. venus in kanya is enjoyment in the sign that inspects everything for faults.

this is genuinely useful, because it means u can reconstruct the reading rather than memorise the table. ask what the sign wants and what the graha wants, and see whether they agree.

tota says

exalted does not mean lucky for u. an exalted graha ruling ur 6th house is expressing debt, illness and rivals extremely effectively. strength is about how well a graha speaks. whether u want to hear it is a different question.

the four signs with neither

Setting aside the disputed node positions, four signs contain no exaltation and no debilitation: Mithuna, Simha, Dhanu and Kumbha. They are ruled by Mercury, the Sun, Jupiter and Saturn.

seven grahas need seven exaltation signs and seven opposite debilitation signs. that is fourteen slots across twelve signs, so some signs get two entries and some get none.

notice which four are empty. mithuna and kanya are mercury's pair, and only kanya carries entries. simha is the sun's only sign. dhanu and meena are jupiter's pair, and only meena carries entries. kumbha and makara are saturn's, and only makara does.

so for each of those grahas, one of its own signs is a busy exaltation slot and the other is empty. that is not chaos, it is a distribution problem being solved.

why the degree matters as much as the sign

Exaltation peaks at one specific degree and tapers away from it. A graha in its exaltation sign but far from the peak degree is exalted in name and much weaker in practice.

the sun peaks at 10 degrees of mesha. a sun at 10 degrees mesha is at maximum exaltation. a sun at 29 degrees mesha is technically exalted and nowhere near its best.

this is why the peak degrees are worth having in front of u, and why the two extremes are so different:

peaks early in the signpeaks late in the sign
Moon at 3° VrishabhaVenus at 27° Meena
Jupiter at 5° KarkaMars at 28° Makara
Sun at 10° MeshaSaturn at 20° Tula

the practical consequence: a moon at 25 degrees vrishabha is a long way from 3 degrees, and calling it strongly exalted overstates it. always ask for the degree, not just the sign. and the same applies in reverse to a debilitation, which is often much shallower than it sounds.

how common a debilitation actually is

Each graha has roughly a one in twelve chance of landing in its debilitation sign. Across the seven visible grahas, the chance that at least one is debilitated works out close to one chart in two.

the arithmetic is quick. if each graha has an eleven in twelve chance of not being debilitated, then seven of them all avoiding it is eleven twelfths multiplied by itself seven times, which is about 0.55. so roughly 45 percent of charts contain at least one debilitated graha.

the same sum applies to exaltation, so about the same share of charts contain at least one exalted graha too.

hold both numbers together and the framing changes completely. these are not rare marks of fortune or misfortune. they are ordinary features that turn up in about half of everybody, and any reading that treats one as decisive has skipped the base rate.

this keeps being the same lesson. we ran it on raj yoga, on nadi dosha and on manglik: find out how common a label is before u find out what it means.

neecha bhanga, and its limits

Neecha bhanga is the cancellation of a debilitation. Several conditions are commonly cited, and different texts list them differently. Some sources go further and read a cancelled debilitation as a source of strength, which is where the name neecha bhanga raja yoga comes from.

the conditions most often given:

  • the lord of the debilitation sign sits in a kendra from the lagna or from the moon.
  • the lord of the graha's exaltation sign sits in a kendra from the lagna or the moon.
  • the debilitated graha is aspected by its own dispositor, the lord of the sign it sits in.
  • the debilitated graha is conjunct or exchanged with its dispositor.
  • the debilitated graha sits in a kendra from the lagna while the lagna lord is strong.

and here is the honest note that usually gets left out: these conditions are broad, and different texts disagree about them. between them they cancel a very large share of debilitations, which means neecha bhanga is common too.

that is not a reason to dismiss it. it is a reason to be sceptical of anyone who announces a debilitation dramatically and does not immediately check the cancellations. the tradition supplies them in the same breath.

mooltrikona is a different thing

Mooltrikona is a degree range inside a graha's own sign where it is especially strong. It is not exaltation, it is not in a different sign, and confusing the two is one of the most common beginner errors.

two oddities worth spotting. the moon's mooltrikona is in vrishabha, which is not a sign it rules, and it is unusually wide at 26 degrees. and mercury's is in kanya from 16 to 20, sitting immediately after its exaltation peak at 15, which makes kanya an unusually loaded stretch of zodiac for budha.

the practical ordering most practitioners use, strongest first: exalted at peak, mooltrikona, own sign, friendly sign, neutral, enemy sign, debilitated. the friendship tables cover the middle of that list.

myths, one at a time

A debilitated planet means something is wrong with me.

About 45 percent of charts contain at least one. It describes a graha in an uncomfortable sign, not an outcome in a life.

Exalted means lucky.

It means the graha expresses itself powerfully. If it rules ur 6th or 8th, it is expressing difficulty powerfully.

Exaltation is the same as being in its own sign.

Different concepts, and usually different signs. Mercury is the single exception where they coincide.

The sign is what matters, not the degree.

Exaltation peaks at one degree and tapers. A sun at 29 degrees mesha is a long way from 10.

Mooltrikona is a kind of exaltation.

It is a strong degree range inside a graha's own sign. Related idea, different mechanism, different sign.

Rahu is definitely exalted in Vrishabha.

Traditions disagree, and the nodes own no signs, so there is no authority to settle it. Anyone stating it flatly is picking a side without saying so.

faq

what does exalted mean in vedic astrology?

uchcha. the sign where a graha functions at its best, with a specific degree where that peaks. exaltation is about how well a graha can express itself, not about whether good things happen to u.

what does debilitated mean?

neecha. the sign where a graha functions least comfortably, again with a specific deepest degree. it is always the sign exactly opposite the exaltation sign.

are exaltation and debilitation always opposite?

always, and at the same degree. the sun is exalted at 10 degrees mesha and debilitated at 10 degrees tula. that pattern holds for every graha without exception.

which signs have no exaltation or debilitation?

mithuna, simha, dhanu and kumbha have neither, if u set the disputed rahu and ketu positions aside. those four are ruled by mercury, the sun, jupiter and saturn respectively.

why is mercury exalted in a sign it rules?

mercury is the only graha where exaltation and rulership coincide, both in kanya. no classical text derives a mechanical reason, so it is best treated as a feature of the system rather than something to explain away.

does the exact degree matter?

yes. a graha exalted by sign but sitting far from the peak degree is not at full strength. the sun at 10 degrees mesha is at its peak. the sun at 29 degrees mesha is exalted by sign and much weaker in practice.

what is neecha bhanga raja yoga?

the cancellation of a debilitation, which some texts then read as a strength. the commonly cited conditions involve the lord of the debilitation sign, or the lord of the graha's exaltation sign, being well placed in a kendra from the lagna or the moon.

how common is a debilitated planet?

much more common than people expect. each graha has roughly a one in twelve chance, so across the seven visible grahas the odds of at least one debilitation land near one chart in two.

is mooltrikona the same as exaltation?

no, and they get confused constantly. mooltrikona is a degree range inside a graha's own sign where it is especially strong. exaltation is a different sign entirely.

where are rahu and ketu exalted?

disputed. the most commonly used positions are rahu exalted in vrishabha and ketu in vrishchika, with the reverse for debilitation. other traditions use mithuna and dhanu. since the nodes own no signs, there is no settled answer.

does a debilitated planet mean bad things happen?

no. it describes a graha operating in an uncomfortable environment, so it expresses itself awkwardly or indirectly. what happens in a life depends on far more, starting with what that graha rules for ur lagna.

is an exalted planet always good for me?

not necessarily. an exalted graha that rules ur 6th or 8th house is expressing difficult significations very effectively. strength and helpfulness are separate questions.
