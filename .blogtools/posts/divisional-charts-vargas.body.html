ur birth chart is a photo of the whole sky. it is a great photo. it is also taken from quite far away.

divisional charts, called vargas, are how vedic astrology zooms in. each one slices every sign into smaller parts, works out which slice each planet sits in, and redraws the chart from that. the d9 zooms in on marriage, the d10 on career, the d12 on parents.

parashara lists sixteen of them. u do not need all sixteen.

the short version

  • a varga divides each 30° sign into equal parts and maps each part to a sign.
  • "d9" means each sign is divided into 9 parts. "d60" means 60.
  • parashara's shodashavarga is sixteen charts, from d1 to d60.
  • the most used: d1 (everything), d9 (marriage, strength), d10 (career).
  • the finer the division, the more birth time precision it needs. d60 changes every 2 minutes.
  • vargas refine the main chart. they do not overrule it.

what a divisional chart is

A divisional chart divides each 30 degree sign into a set number of equal parts and assigns each part to a sign by a fixed rule. Each planet and the ascendant is placed in the sign of the part it falls in, producing a new chart. Each varga is used to examine one specific area of life.

an analogy: the main chart says which city a planet lives in. the d9 says which neighbourhood. the d60 says which building. the planet has not moved. u are just reading its address at a finer level.

the simplest example is the d2, hora. each sign is split into two halves of 15°. in odd signs the first half is the sun's (leo) and the second the moon's (cancer); in even signs it is the reverse. so every planet ends up in either leo or cancer in the d2, and that is read for wealth.

the sixteen vargas

Parashara's sixteen divisional charts are D1, D2, D3, D4, D7, D9, D10, D12, D16, D20, D24, D27, D30, D40, D45 and D60. Each covers a life area, from wealth in the D2 and siblings in the D3 to education in the D24 and past karma in the D60.

the big three: d1, d9, d10

Most astrologers rely on three charts. The D1 shows the whole life and is always read first. The D9 navamsa confirms planetary strength and is the main chart for marriage. The D10 dashamsa is consulted for career. Together they cover most questions people bring to a reading.

the d9 deserves its reputation. a planet exalted in the d1 but debilitated in the d9 is read as looking strong while lacking inner support. a planet weak in the d1 but strong in the d9 is read as improving with time.

how much birth time each needs

The ascendant moves about one degree every four minutes on average. A varga's ascendant changes whenever the D1 ascendant crosses one of its divisions. So the D9 ascendant changes roughly every 13 minutes, the D10 every 12, and the D60 about every 2 minutes. Finer charts need more precise birth times.

these are averages. signs rise faster or slower depending on latitude and the sign itself. but the lesson holds: a birth time rounded to the nearest 15 minutes makes the d9 ascendant uncertain and the d60 meaningless. more in why ur birth time matters.

planets are much less sensitive than the ascendant. even the fast moon moves only about half a degree an hour, so its d9 sign is usually stable unless it sits near a boundary.

how vargas are read together

The classical rule is that the D1 shows what is promised, and the divisional charts show how fully that promise is delivered in each area. A varga can strengthen or weaken an indication from the D1, but cannot create something the D1 does not show. Planets are also scored across vargas using vimshopaka bala.

so a strong 10th house in the d1 with a weak d10 might be read as real career potential with obstacles in fulfilling it. a weak 10th in the d1 with a strong d10 as modest public life but satisfying work. the d1 sets the outline, the varga fills in the detail.

vargottama is the most common cross-chart check: a planet in the same sign in d1 and d9 is read as consistent and strong. see nakshatra padas for exactly where it happens.

where a beginner should start

Start with the D1 and the D9. Learn to read the main chart first, then check each important planet's sign in the navamsa. Add the D10 for career questions. Leave the higher vargas until your birth time is verified and your D1 reading is confident.

  1. read the d1 properly. how to read a kundli walks through it.
  2. check the d9. note which planets are stronger or weaker there.
  3. look for vargottama planets. they are anchors in the chart.
  4. add the d10 if career is the question.
  5. verify ur birth time before anything finer.

myths, one at a time

Divisional charts are separate charts with their own predictions.

They are finer readings of the same planetary positions, and they refine the D1 rather than replace it.

The D60 is the most important chart, so use it first.

Parashara weights it highly, but it changes every two minutes. Without a verified birth time, it is noise.

A bad D9 ruins a good D1.

The D9 modifies how fully the D1's promise is delivered. It does not erase it.

You need all sixteen charts for a reading.

Most practical readings use the D1, D9 and D10. The rest are specialist tools for specific questions.

faq

what are divisional charts in vedic astrology?

charts made by dividing each 30 degree sign into equal parts and mapping each part to a sign. every planet's position in those small parts is redrawn as a new chart. each divisional chart is used to look closely at one area of life.

how many divisional charts are there?

brihat parashara hora shastra describes sixteen, called the shodashavarga: d1, d2, d3, d4, d7, d9, d10, d12, d16, d20, d24, d27, d30, d40, d45 and d60. some traditions use additional ones.

which divisional chart is most important?

after the d1 birth chart, the d9 navamsa is treated as the most important. it is used for marriage and for judging the real strength of every planet. the d10 for career is the next most consulted.

what does d9 show?

the navamsa shows marriage and the spouse, dharma, and the inner strength of planets. a planet strong in d1 but weak in d9 is read as promising more than it delivers.

what does d10 show?

the dashamsa shows career, profession, status and achievements in public life. it is read alongside the 10th house of the main chart.

what does d60 show?

the shashtiamsa is linked to past life karma and parashara gives it the highest weight among the vargas. it changes about every two minutes of birth time, so it is only usable with a very precise birth time.

how accurate does my birth time need to be for divisional charts?

it depends on the chart. the d9 lagna changes roughly every 13 minutes on average, the d10 every 12, and the d60 every 2. if ur birth time is rounded to the nearest half hour, trust the d1 and use the finer vargas carefully.

what is vargottama?

a planet occupying the same sign in the d1 and the d9. it is traditionally read as a sign of strength and consistency.

what is d7 chart used for?

the saptamsa is used for children and progeny, and more broadly for creative output and legacy.

what is the d30 chart?

the trimshamsa, used to examine misfortunes, health vulnerabilities and character flaws. unlike most vargas, its divisions are unequal, ruled by mars, saturn, jupiter, mercury and venus.

do i need divisional charts as a beginner?

start with the d1 and the d9. the d1 shows the whole picture and the d9 checks it. add the d10 if career is the question. the higher vargas are specialist tools.

can divisional charts contradict the main chart?

they refine it rather than overrule it. the classical rule is that a varga can modify what the d1 promises, but cannot create something the d1 does not show at all.
