27 nakshatras, ranked by how messy they are

ur rashi is a 12-sign zodiac. ur nakshatra is a finer, 27-part one underneath it, and it's the one Vedic astrology actually times things off. here's what it is, and how to find urs.

AUGUST 10, 2026 · 9 MIN READ

ask an Indian astrologer for ur rashi and they'll usually ask a second question right after: what's ur nakshatra. two different tools, two different jobs, and most people only ever learn about the first one.

rashi is the 12-sign zodiac everyone knows, each sign 30 degrees wide. nakshatra is a 27-part zodiac laid on top of the same sky, each one 13°20' wide. it's not a simplification or a variant. it's the system Vedic astrology actually uses to name things, time things, and match people.

rashi and nakshatra are measuring the same sky, differently

both systems divide the same 360-degree band of sky the moon, sun and planets move through. rashi cuts it into 12 equal pieces of 30 degrees, named after constellations: Mesha, Vrishabha, Mithuna, and so on. nakshatra cuts the identical 360 degrees into 27 equal pieces of 13°20' each, named after specific stars or small star groups: Ashwini, Bharani, Krittika, and 24 more.

every degree of sky belongs to exactly one rashi and exactly one nakshatra at the same time. they're not competing systems, they're two different rulers laid over the same measurement.

why 27, specifically

27 isn't an arbitrary round number. it comes from the moon. the moon takes about 27.3 days to complete one full circuit of the zodiac relative to the stars, a sidereal month. divide the zodiac into 27 pieces and the moon spends almost exactly one day in each one.

that's the entire design logic: nakshatra is the moon's daily address. ancient skywatchers tracking the moon night to night needed a system with roughly one stop per night, and 27 (sometimes historically 28) nakshatras is what that produces.

this is also why nakshatra is the system dasha timing runs on, not rashi. vimshottari dasha starts from the specific nakshatra your moon occupied at birth, because that's the finer-grained clock the tradition already had.

what a nakshatra actually carries

each of the 27 nakshatras has a ruling planet, assigned in a fixed, repeating nine-planet sequence: Ketu, Venus, Sun, Moon, Mars, Rahu, Jupiter, Saturn, Mercury, then the cycle repeats. nine planets into 27 nakshatras divides evenly, three full cycles.

that ruling planet is not decorative. it's the same nine-planet list vimshottari dasha runs on, and it's specifically your moon's nakshatra lord that decides which planet's dasha u start life in.

each nakshatra also carries a gana (temperament: deva, manushya, or rakshasa), a yoni (an animal symbol used in compatibility matching), and a nadi (one of three humors used in the same matching system). all three feed directly into ashtakoota, the traditional compatibility framework — more on that in how ashtakoota actually scores compatibility.

padas: the quarter-nakshatra

each nakshatra is further split into four padas, or quarters, of 3°20' each. this is the level naming traditions in many indian families still work from: the specific pada a birth falls in maps to a syllable, and that syllable is meant to be the first sound of the child's name.

108 padas total across 27 nakshatras (27 × 4), each tied to one of the 108 traditional naming syllables. it's the most granular level most family astrology practice actually uses day to day.

why this needs a real birth time

the moon moves through an entire nakshatra, 13°20', in roughly one day. that sounds forgiving until u do the math on padas: each pada is 3°20', which the moon crosses in about six hours. a birth time that's off by a couple of hours can genuinely land u in the wrong pada, and if ur birthday falls near a nakshatra boundary, it can land u in the wrong nakshatra entirely.

this is the same sensitivity we've written about for the moon sign in general, just one level more granular. rashi tolerates an approximate time. nakshatra and pada do not.

the receipt

the 27-fold division, the 13°20' width, and the nine-planet ruling sequence are the standard classical definition of nakshatra, not a simplification. the tie to the moon's 27.3-day sidereal cycle is straightforward orbital mechanics. what's worth being upfront about: a handful of classical sources describe a 28th nakshatra, Abhijit, inserted for specific muhurat calculations. it's not part of the standard 27-nakshatra dasha or compatibility system, which is what this tool and vimshottari dasha both use.

faq

what is a nakshatra?

one of 27 equal 13°20' divisions of the zodiac, based on the moon's roughly 27.3-day sidereal orbit. it's the finer-grained system underneath the 12-sign rashi zodiac.

why are there 27 nakshatras and not some other number?

27 divides the moon's ~27.3-day sidereal month into close to one nakshatra per day, which is the system's original design purpose.

is my nakshatra the same as my moon sign?

no. moon sign (rashi) is one of 12 broad 30-degree signs. nakshatra is one of 27 finer 13°20' divisions. your moon sits in exactly one of each at the same time.

what is a pada?

a quarter of a nakshatra, 3°20' wide. 27 nakshatras × 4 padas = 108 total, each traditionally tied to a naming syllable.

do I need my exact birth time to find my nakshatra?

yes, ideally. the moon crosses an entire nakshatra in about a day and a pada in about six hours, so an approximate time can land u in the wrong one.
