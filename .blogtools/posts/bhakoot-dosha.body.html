the matching report comes back and one line says bhakoot: 0/7. somebody's aunt goes quiet.

bhakoot dosha sounds like a rare problem. it is not. it comes from one simple measurement, how far apart two moon signs are, and three of the six possible distance pairs score zero. that means roughly half of all random couples have it before anyone checks the cancellation rules.

the short version

  • bhakoot is the 7 point koota in ashtakoota guna milan.
  • it scores 7 or 0, from the distance between the two moon signs.
  • 0 points when the moons are 2/12, 5/9 or 6/8 apart. that is bhakoot dosha.
  • 6 of 12 distances are dosha, so about half of random couples have it.
  • it is cancelled when both moon signs share a lord, and often when the lords are friends.
  • it is one factor in one system. a full reading compares the whole charts.

what bhakoot measures

Bhakoot koota compares the positions of two people's natal Moon signs. Counting from one Moon sign to the other gives a pair of numbers, such as 3 and 11. Favourable pairs score the full 7 points; the 2/12, 5/9 and 6/8 pairs score zero, which is called bhakoot dosha.

the counting is always done both ways, which is why the results come in pairs. if her moon is in mesha (aries) and his is in mithuna (gemini), mithuna is 3rd from mesha and mesha is 11th from mithuna. that is a 3/11 relationship, full marks.

the three types of dosha

Bhakoot dosha has three forms. Dwirdwadash (2/12), where the Moon signs are adjacent, is traditionally linked to finances. Navam-pancham (5/9) is linked to children. Shadashtak (6/8) is linked to friction and health and is usually treated as the most serious. These are traditional associations, not predictions.

navam-pancham is the interesting one. 5 and 9 are trines, which are the most harmonious relationship almost everywhere else in astrology. many astrologers treat the 5/9 dosha as minor for exactly that reason, and some ignore it.

why half of couples have it

Counting from one Moon sign, the other can be in any of twelve positions. Positions 2, 12, 5, 9, 6 and 8 all produce bhakoot dosha. That is six out of twelve, so a random couple has roughly a fifty percent chance of bhakoot dosha before any cancellation rule is applied.

it is worth sitting with that number. a "dosha" that turns up in half of all pairings cannot sensibly mean that half of all pairings are doomed. the tradition clearly did not think so either, which is why the cancellation rules are broad.

how bhakoot dosha is cancelled

The most widely accepted cancellation is when both Moon signs share a ruling planet. Many traditions also cancel it when the two sign lords are natural friends, when the navamsa signs of the Moons are compatible, or when the other kootas score strongly. Rules vary by school and text.

add these together and a large share of the original fifty percent is cancelled. the exact share depends on which rules an astrologer accepts, which is itself a sign of how flexible this koota is.

how much it should weigh

Bhakoot is one of eight kootas, and koota matching compares only the two Moons. A thorough compatibility reading also compares the 7th houses, Venus, Mars, dashas and the overall strength of both charts. No controlled research shows koota scores predict marriage outcomes, so bhakoot is best treated as one prompt among many.

the ashtakoota system is a filter that families have used for centuries to start a conversation. it is a moon to moon comparison, and the moon is one graha of nine. reading a whole relationship from one koota is like judging a house by one window.

faq

what is bhakoot dosha?

a zero score in bhakoot, the 7 point koota of ashtakoota guna milan. it happens when the two partners' moon signs are 2 and 12, 5 and 9, or 6 and 8 signs apart counted from each other.

how many points is bhakoot worth?

7 out of 36. it is the second highest koota after nadi, which is worth 8. bhakoot scores either 7 or 0, with no partial scores in the standard system.

how common is bhakoot dosha?

very common. of the twelve possible distances between two moon signs, six fall into the dosha pairs. so roughly half of random couples have bhakoot dosha before cancellations are checked.

what is shadashtak dosha?

the 6/8 form of bhakoot dosha, where the moon signs are six and eight signs apart. it is traditionally treated as the most serious of the three and linked to friction and health concerns.

what is navam pancham dosha?

the 5/9 form, with moon signs five and nine apart. traditionally linked to matters around children. many astrologers treat it as the mildest, since 5 and 9 are trines that are favourable elsewhere in astrology.

what is dwirdwadash dosha?

the 2/12 form, with moon signs two and twelve apart, meaning adjacent signs. traditionally linked to finances and expenses.

how is bhakoot dosha cancelled?

common cancellations: both moon signs ruled by the same planet, such as aries and scorpio under mars; the two sign lords being natural friends; and in some traditions, a strong score in other kootas or a friendly navamsa relationship.

can i marry with bhakoot dosha?

many couples do. classical texts list cancellations, and even uncancelled, bhakoot is one factor in a matching system that itself is one part of chart comparison. a full reading looks at both charts, not just the koota score.

is bhakoot dosha worse than nadi dosha?

nadi carries 8 points and is usually treated as weightier. both are binary kootas that score full or zero. both have classical cancellation rules.

what is a good bhakoot relationship?

same sign, 3/11, 4/10, or 7/7. these score the full 7. the 7/7 relationship, moons in opposite signs, is often read as especially complementary.

does bhakoot dosha apply if we have the same moon sign?

no. the same moon sign is a 1/1 relationship, which scores the full 7 points for bhakoot. other kootas such as nadi may still need checking.

is bhakoot dosha a real problem?

it is a traditional indicator within a traditional system. no controlled research shows koota scores predict marriage outcomes. treat it as one prompt for conversation, not a verdict on a relationship.
