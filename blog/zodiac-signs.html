the twelve rashis, and why urs probably moved

same twelve signs, different starting line. here is every rashi with its ruler, element and actual sidereal dates, why ur sign is usually one behind the western one, and why nobody in vedic astrology is born on a cusp.

JULY 26, 2026 · 13 MIN READ

u already know ur zodiac sign. there is a decent chance it is not the one Vedic astrology would give u.

the signs themselves are the same twelve. same names, same order, same thirty degrees each. what differs is where the counting starts, and that difference is about twenty-four degrees, which is most of a sign. this piece gives u all twelve rashis with their rulers, elements and the sidereal date ranges that actually apply, plus a calculator that will tell u ur moon sign, which is the one that matters most here.

the short version

  • the twelve signs in Vedic astrology are called rashis and carry Sanskrit names: Mesha, Vrishabha, Mithuna, Karka, Simha, Kanya, Tula, Vrishchika, Dhanu, Makara, Kumbha, Meena.
  • they map one to one onto Aries through Pisces. same signs, different starting point.
  • because the Vedic zodiac is fixed to the stars, the date ranges sit roughly three weeks later than the western ones.
  • if u were born in about the last three weeks of ur western sign, ur Vedic sun sign is the previous one.
  • in India, ur rashi means ur moon sign, not ur sun sign. this is the single most useful thing on this page.
  • cusp signs do not exist in Vedic astrology. a degree belongs to exactly one sign.

why ur sign moved

Western astrology counts from the spring equinox. Vedic astrology counts from a fixed point among the stars. Those two starting lines have drifted about 24 degrees apart since they last coincided around 285 CE, which pushes each Vedic sign roughly three weeks later than its western twin.

this is the whole story in one paragraph, and everything else on this page follows from it.

the earth wobbles on its axis like a slowing spinning top, one full circle every 25,772 years. that wobble drags the equinox slowly backwards against the stars, about 50.29 arcseconds a year, which comes to roughly one degree every 72 years. this is precession, it is ordinary astronomy, and Hipparchus wrote it down in the second century BCE.

western astrology pinned its zero point to the equinox, so its zodiac travels with the wobble. Vedic astrology pinned its zero point to the stars, so its zodiac stays put. the two agreed once, around 285 CE. they have been separating ever since, and the current gap, called the ayanamsa, sits at about 24 degrees 13 minutes.

24 degrees out of a 30 degree sign is a lot. it means most people born in the back end of their western sign are a different sign in Vedic terms. someone who has called themselves a Sagittarius since school, born on 12 December, is a Vedic Vrishchika. we worked through the arithmetic properly in why ur sun sign is different in Vedic astrology.

the twelve, one at a time

Each rashi is read through its ruling graha, its element and its modality. The ruler does the most work: a Mesha placement is read through Mars, a Tula placement through Venus, and so on. The element sets the temperament and the modality sets how it handles change.

Mesha (Aries), ruled by Mars

fire, movable. the starting sign, and it behaves like one. initiative, impatience, a preference for doing over discussing. the sun is exalted here, which is the tradition saying that authority expresses cleanly in a sign built for going first.

Vrishabha (Taurus), ruled by Venus

earth, fixed. stability, taste, patience, and a strong preference for not being rushed. the moon is exalted here, which fits: emotional life is steadiest in a sign that likes things to stay where they are put.

Mithuna (Gemini), ruled by Mercury

air, dual. speech, curiosity, quick switching, a talent for holding two ideas at once. dual signs handle change by adapting rather than resisting it, which reads as flexible on a good day and unanchored on a bad one.

Karka (Cancer), ruled by the Moon

water, movable. memory, protection, home, and a long emotional recall. Jupiter is exalted here. the combination of the moon's rulership and Jupiter's exaltation makes this the most reliably nurturing sign in the system.

Simha (Leo), ruled by the Sun

fire, fixed. authority, warmth, visibility, and a genuine need to be seen doing the thing. the only sign with a single ruler that rules nothing else, which the tradition treats as significant: the sun has one house and holds it absolutely.

Kanya (Virgo), ruled by Mercury

earth, dual. analysis, service, precision, and a low tolerance for sloppiness. Mercury is both ruler and exalted here, the only graha with that double claim on any sign, which is why Kanya placements tend to read as unusually sharp.

Tula (Libra), ruled by Venus

air, movable. balance, negotiation, aesthetics, and a real discomfort with unresolved conflict. Saturn is exalted here, which surprises people until u notice that Saturn's job is fairness applied consistently over time.

Vrishchika (Scorpio), ruled by Mars

water, fixed. depth, secrecy, endurance, and an appetite for what is underneath. the moon is debilitated here, which the tradition reads as emotional life running deep and uncomfortably rather than badly.

Dhanu (Sagittarius), ruled by Jupiter

fire, dual. belief, teaching, travel, philosophy, and a tendency to speak in principles. Jupiter's own sign, so the expansive, ethical, slightly preachy register runs at full strength.

Makara (Capricorn), ruled by Saturn

earth, movable. structure, ambition, patience, and a comfort with long timescales. Mars is exalted here, which is the tradition pairing raw drive with the one sign that will make it wait.

Kumbha (Aquarius), ruled by Saturn

air, fixed. systems, groups, detachment, and a genuine interest in how things ought to be arranged. Saturn's other sign, and the more intellectual of the two.

Meena (Pisces), ruled by Jupiter

water, dual. dissolution, empathy, imagination, and porous boundaries. Venus is exalted here, which reads as love without conditions attached, for better and worse.

why cusp signs do not exist here

In Vedic astrology every degree of the zodiac belongs to exactly one sign. There is no shared border zone. If the sun is at 29 degrees 59 minutes of Mesha, it is in Mesha, fully, with no Vrishabha in it. The cusp idea comes from western popular astrology and has no Vedic equivalent.

people born near a date boundary often say they are a cusp sign, half one and half the next. it is a comforting idea and it is not part of this system.

the reason is structural. Vedic astrology is built on exact degrees, and it subdivides constantly: each sign into 30 degrees, the zodiac into 27 nakshatras, each nakshatra into four padas, and each sign again into nine navamsa parts. a system that slices this finely cannot also have fuzzy borders. the two ideas are incompatible.

what is true, and what the cusp idea is probably reaching for, is that near a boundary ur birth time matters more. if the sun was at 29 degrees 50 minutes when u were born, then a birth time recorded ten minutes wrong could genuinely put u in the next sign. that is a data precision problem, not a blended identity.

the honest thing to do near a boundary is to say so. our calculators flag it when a result lands close to an edge rather than quietly picking one, because a confident wrong answer is worse than an uncertain right one.

which sign is actually urs

You have at least three, and they usually differ. Ur moon sign is ur rashi and is what Indian practice means by ur sign. Ur rising sign sets the house structure of the whole chart. Ur sun sign matters least here, which is the reverse of western popular astrology.

this is also why Indian horoscopes in newspapers are printed by moon sign rather than sun sign. it is not a different convention for its own sake. it is a different reading of which placement carries the personal weight. sun sign vs moon sign vs rising sign goes through all three properly.

sign compatibility, and why it is the weakest tool here

Vedic astrology does not primarily match people by zodiac sign. It matches birth nakshatras through the ashtakoota system, scoring eight factors out of a total of 36. Sign-to-sign compatibility is a rough shorthand at best and is not what a traditional matchmaker uses.

sign compatibility charts are everywhere, and in Vedic terms they are the shallowest possible version of the question.

what the tradition actually uses is ashtakoota guna milan: eight tests run on the two birth nakshatras, weighted unevenly, totalling 36 points. one of those eight tests, Nadi, carries 8 points by itself. another, Bhakoot, is worth 7 and does look at the moon signs, so sign compatibility is in there, but as a fraction of the whole rather than the whole thing.

the practical implication is that two people whose sun signs are supposedly incompatible can score well, and two whose sun signs are supposedly perfect can score badly. if a compatibility answer was produced from two sun signs alone, it did not use the Vedic method at all. we broke the whole system down in how ashtakoota scores compatibility out of 36.

what gets repeated, and what is actually true

My zodiac sign changed, so one of the two systems is wrong.

Both systems agree exactly on where the sun was. They disagree about what to call that spot, because they count from different zero points. It is a naming difference, not a factual one.

NASA changed the zodiac and added Ophiuchus.

NASA published an explainer about constellation boundaries versus zodiac signs for a schools audience and did not change anything. Astrology has used twelve equal 30 degree divisions for well over two thousand years, and constellations were never equal in size.

I am a cusp sign, so I am a bit of both.

Not in this system. Every degree belongs to one sign. What is true is that near a boundary ur birth time needs to be accurate, because ten minutes can move the answer.

My rashi is my sun sign.

In Indian usage rashi almost always means the moon sign. This one misunderstanding is behind a lot of people concluding that Vedic astrology does not work for them.

Fire signs and water signs cannot get along.

Element pairing is a broad and unreliable shorthand, and it is not what Vedic matchmaking uses. Ashtakoota scores eight separate factors from the two birth nakshatras.

mistakes worth not making

  • using western sign dates with Vedic meanings. the date ranges shift by about three weeks. mixing the two gives u the wrong sign and then confident interpretations of it.
  • reading ur sun sign and stopping. in this system it is the least personal of ur three main placements.
  • assuming the Sanskrit names are just translations. they are, but the sign meanings carry different emphasis. Vrishchika is not a carbon copy of the western Scorpio.
  • trusting a date table near a boundary. sign entry dates shift a day either way by year. calculate rather than look up if u were born close to one.
  • judging compatibility on sun signs. the Vedic method reads nakshatras, not signs, and weights eight factors unequally.

the receipt

the sidereal date ranges given are calculated on the Lahiri ayanamsa and shift by about a day either side depending on the year, and very slowly across centuries as the ayanamsa grows. we have given them as approximate rather than fixed because that is what they are.

the precession figures, a 25,772 year cycle and about 50.29 arcseconds a year, are standard astronomy independent of any astrological claim. the Lahiri ayanamsa was fixed by India's Calendar Reform Committee in 1955 under Meghnad Saha and remains the government standard.

rulerships, exaltations and the element and modality groupings follow Brihat Parashara Hora Shastra and Brihat Jataka. the sign character sketches are traditional interpretation rather than measurement, and we would rather label that plainly than blur it into the arithmetic.

frequently asked questions

what are the 12 zodiac signs in Vedic astrology?

Mesha, Vrishabha, Mithuna, Karka, Simha, Kanya, Tula, Vrishchika, Dhanu, Makara, Kumbha and Meena. They map one to one onto Aries through Pisces.

why is my Vedic zodiac sign different?

because the Vedic zodiac is fixed to the stars while the western one is fixed to the equinox. The two have drifted about 24 degrees apart, so if u were born in roughly the last three weeks of ur western sign, ur Vedic sun sign is the one before.

what is a rashi?

a rashi is one of the twelve signs. In everyday Indian use, ur rashi means ur moon sign, not ur sun sign, which is the more important distinction.

what is my moon sign?

the sign the moon was in at ur birth. It changes roughly every two and a quarter days, so u need ur birth date and a reasonably accurate time. The calculator on this page will find it.

which zodiac sign is the best?

none. Each sign is a set of tendencies, and every sign contains people doing well and people struggling. Any source ranking signs by quality is writing entertainment.

what are cusp signs in Vedic astrology?

there are none. Every degree belongs to exactly one sign. Near a boundary, ur birth time simply needs to be accurate.

who rules each zodiac sign?

Mars rules Mesha and Vrishchika, Venus rules Vrishabha and Tula, Mercury rules Mithuna and Kanya, the Moon rules Karka, the Sun rules Simha, Jupiter rules Dhanu and Meena, and Saturn rules Makara and Kumbha.

what are the elements of the zodiac signs?

fire, earth, air and water, repeating in that order. Mesha is fire, Vrishabha earth, Mithuna air, Karka water, and so on around the wheel.

what are movable, fixed and dual signs?

the three modalities. Movable signs (Mesha, Karka, Tula, Makara) start things. Fixed signs (Vrishabha, Simha, Vrishchika, Kumbha) hold them. Dual signs (Mithuna, Kanya, Dhanu, Meena) adapt.

did NASA change the zodiac signs?

no. NASA published a schools explainer about the difference between constellations and zodiac signs. Astrology has used twelve equal thirty degree divisions for over two thousand years and constellations were never equal in size.

which zodiac signs are compatible?

Vedic astrology does not answer this from signs alone. It uses ashtakoota, which scores two birth nakshatras across eight weighted factors out of 36. Sign pairing is a fraction of that.

how do I know my Vedic sun sign?

check ur birth date against the sidereal date ranges in the table above, and calculate if u were born within a couple of days of a boundary. The calculator on this page gives it exactly.

is my rashi the same as my nakshatra?

no. Ur rashi is one of twelve signs, thirty degrees wide. Ur nakshatra is one of twenty-seven divisions, 13 degrees 20 minutes wide. Both are read from the moon's position.

why do Indian newspapers print horoscopes by moon sign?

because Indian practice treats the moon as the personal placement. The sun sign changes once a month and is shared by a twelfth of everyone alive.

can my zodiac sign change during my life?

no. Ur birth placements are fixed. What changes is where the planets are transiting now and which dasha period u are in.

the five things worth remembering

  1. same twelve signs, different starting line. the roughly 24 degree ayanamsa pushes every Vedic sign about three weeks later than its western twin.
  2. ur rashi means ur moon sign in Indian usage. this one correction resolves most of the confusion people have with Vedic astrology.
  3. cusp signs do not exist here. every degree belongs to one sign, and a boundary result is a precision problem rather than a blend.
  4. the ruling planet does more work than the sign itself. reading a Tula placement means reading Venus.
  5. sign compatibility is the weakest tool in the box. the tradition matches nakshatras across eight weighted factors, not signs.
