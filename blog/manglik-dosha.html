manglik dosha is a house number, not a sentence

mars in one of six houses. that is the entire definition. here is how common it actually is, why two astrologers can give u opposite answers about the same chart, what the classical cancellations are, and what is being sold to u in its name.

AUGUST 13, 2026 · 12 MIN READ

few phrases in Indian astrology carry more weight with less explanation than "she's manglik." it gets said in the tone of a diagnosis, and it usually arrives without anyone saying what was measured.

so here is the measurement. manglik dosha means Mars was in the 1st, 2nd, 4th, 7th, 8th or 12th house of a chart. that is the whole definition. everything else, the severity, the marriage warnings, the remedies, is interpretation stacked on top of one house number, and this piece separates the two.

the short version

  • manglik dosha, also called mangal dosha, kuja dosha or chevvai dosham, means Mars sits in the 1st, 2nd, 4th, 7th, 8th or 12th house.
  • it is common. six houses out of twelve is half the chart before u account for how astrologers count, which is why estimates land anywhere from a quarter to a half of all charts.
  • astrologers count from different reference points: the lagna, the moon, or Venus. this alone explains most disagreements about a single chart.
  • classical texts list cancellations, called mangal dosha bhanga, which regularly apply. a proper reading checks these before saying anything.
  • the strongest empirical point against the severe version: if both people are manglik, the tradition itself says it cancels. a defect that neutralises itself when doubled is a compatibility rule, not a curse.
  • no controlled study has linked manglik status to marital outcomes. treat scary claims priced in rupees with the suspicion they deserve.

what manglik dosha actually is

Manglik dosha is the label for Mars occupying the 1st, 2nd, 4th, 7th, 8th or 12th house of a birth chart. Those six houses are grouped because classical texts associate them with the body, family, home, marriage, upheaval and loss, all areas Mars is thought to disturb.

Mars in this tradition rules nerve, temper, competition, physical energy and anything sharp. it is a natural malefic, meaning difficult and demanding rather than evil.

the six houses in the list are not random. the classical reasoning runs like this:

1stbody, temperamenta short fuse aimed at the partner
2ndfamily, speech, moneysharp speech, friction with the extended family
4thhome, emotional basedomestic peace disturbed
7thmarriage itselfconflict placed directly in the marriage house
8thupheaval, longevity, joint assetsthe most severe reading, historically tied to widowhood fears
12thexpenditure, the bed, separationwithdrawal and distance in the marriage

whatever u make of the reasoning, notice what it is: a positional rule producing a category. it is not a measurement of a person's temperament, a prediction of an event, or a statement about anyone's character.

how common is it really

Six of twelve houses is half the chart. If Mars were evenly distributed, the base rate from the lagna alone would be around 50 percent. Real distributions and stricter definitions bring commonly quoted figures down to roughly a quarter to a third, which is still enormous.

this is the number that should reframe the whole conversation, and it is almost never mentioned when someone gets told they are manglik.

start with the arithmetic. the dosha is defined by Mars being in six of the twelve houses. that is half of the possible positions. counting from the lagna alone gets u a base rate in the region of a half, and stricter definitions that require the 2nd house to be excluded, or that require Mars to be un-cancelled, bring it down. published estimates land in the range of a quarter to a half depending on whose definition is used.

now consider what happens when u count from three reference points instead of one, as many astrologers do. checking from the lagna, the moon and Venus and calling someone manglik if any of the three qualifies pushes the proportion up substantially, because each additional count is another chance to hit six houses out of twelve.

the point is not that the tradition is wrong to have the category. it is that a label applying to somewhere between a quarter and a half of everyone alive is a classification, not a diagnosis. if a third of the people u know are manglik, and a third of them are happily married, the severe reading has a problem it needs to explain.

why two astrologers give u opposite answers

Because they count from different reference points and use different house lists. One counts Mars from the lagna, another from the moon, a third from Venus. North Indian practice usually includes the 2nd house, some southern practice does not. Same chart, different method, different answer.

this is the single most useful thing to know before anyone pronounces on ur chart, and it explains almost every case of two astrologers disagreeing about a single person.

the reference point

counting houses from the lagna is the most common. counting from the natal moon is a well-established second method. counting from Venus, as the significator of marriage, is used in some schools. Mars can easily land in a counted house from one reference and not from another. our calculator prints all three, because picking one and calling it the answer would be pretending a live disagreement is settled.

the house list

the 1st, 4th, 7th, 8th and 12th are near universal. the 2nd house is contested: north Indian practice generally includes it, and several southern traditions do not. that one house is the difference between manglik and not for a meaningful slice of people.

the severity grading

some practitioners grade by house, treating the 7th and 8th as severe and the 2nd and 12th as mild. others treat all six equally. this is a judgment call, not a rule, and it is rarely disclosed as one.

if someone tells u u are manglik, the useful follow-up questions are: counted from what, and does ur list include the 2nd house. u will learn more from those two answers than from any amount of discussion about what it means.

the cancellations, which nobody mentions first

Classical texts list conditions under which manglik dosha does not apply, known as mangal dosha bhanga. These include Mars in its own sign or exaltation, Jupiter aspecting the 7th house, Mars conjunct certain planets, and both partners being manglik.

the cancellations are part of the same classical tradition that defines the dosha. a reading that names the dosha and stops before checking them has done half the procedure.

the widely used ones:

  • Mars in its own sign or exaltation. Mars in Mesha, Vrishchika or Makara is generally read as the dosha standing down, because a planet operating in comfortable conditions is not being disruptive.
  • Mars in Karka or Simha. several schools treat these as neutralising, though not all agree.
  • Jupiter aspecting or occupying the 7th house. the classical great benefic looking after the marriage house is one of the most commonly cited cancellations.
  • Mars conjunct or aspected by Saturn, Rahu or Ketu. treated as tempering by several sources.
  • both partners manglik. the best known cancellation and the most revealing one.
  • Mars in specific house and sign combinations, for example Mars in the 7th in Makara or Karka, which classical sources list individually.

the different sources do not fully agree on this list either, which is worth being honest about. what they agree on is that cancellations exist and are not rare. any reading that goes straight from the label to a remedy has skipped a step the tradition itself requires.

the both-manglik rule, and what it gives away

Classical practice holds that if both partners are manglik, the dosha cancels and the match is considered acceptable. This is the single most informative rule in the whole subject, because a genuine defect in one person cannot be neutralised by the same defect in another.

sit with the logic of it for a second. if manglik dosha were a real property of one person that damaged marriages, pairing two of them would produce more damage, not less.

it does not work that way, and the tradition says so. two mangliks are considered a suitable match, and the dosha is treated as cancelled.

which tells u what the rule actually is. it is not a statement about a person. it is a matching heuristic: a way of pairing people with similar chart signatures within a system designed to sort large numbers of introductions quickly. read that way it is coherent, and it stops being frightening.

it also puts the commercial version in an awkward position. if two mangliks marrying is fine, then being manglik is not a defect requiring an expensive fix. it is a preference to be matched around, like any other.

what is actually being sold

The manglik label is one of the most commercially exploited concepts in Indian astrology. The pattern is consistent: name the fear, imply a marriage is at risk, and offer a paid remedy as the way out. The label itself is free and calculable from any chart.

we would rather say this plainly than let it sit implied.

the sales pattern is easy to spot once u know it. the reading opens with a severity claim, usually framed around marriage. it does not mention how common the dosha is. it does not mention which reference point was used. it does not check the cancellations. and it arrives with a specific priced remedy in the same conversation.

the most widely marketed of these is kumbh vivah, a ceremonial marriage to a pot, a tree or an idol, said to absorb the dosha. it is a genuine folk practice with real regional history. it is also sold at prices that have nothing to do with folk practice, to people who were never told the dosha applies to a substantial fraction of the population.

three questions worth asking before u pay for anything:

  1. counted from the lagna, the moon or Venus, and does the list include the 2nd house?
  2. which cancellations were checked, and what did they show?
  3. what happens if I do nothing?

a practitioner working in good faith will answer all three without irritation. the answers are also exactly what a sales script cannot survive.

so what should u actually do about it

Find out which reference point was used, check the cancellations, and treat the label as one factor in a matching conversation rather than a verdict. If the manglik question is being used to pressure you, the problem is the pressure and not the chart.

practical, in order:

  1. get the actual placement. which house is Mars in, counted from what. a real number, not a label. the calculator on this page will give it to u for free.
  2. check the cancellations. if one applies, the classical tradition itself says the question is closed.
  3. put it in proportion. ashtakoota matching scores 36 points across eight factors and manglik status is not one of them. it is a separate check that sits alongside, and it is one input among many.
  4. separate the astrology from the pressure. in a lot of situations where manglik comes up, the chart is being used as a socially acceptable way to say something else. the chart is not the actual conversation.
  5. do not pay to fix a category. if a remedy is offered before the reference point and the cancellations have been discussed, u are being sold to.

and if u are the one who has just found out u are manglik: somewhere between a quarter and a half of the people u know are too. most of them have no idea. it has not visibly ruined their lives.

what gets said about manglik dosha, and what is actually the case

A manglik person will harm or outlive their spouse.

This is the severe historical reading of Mars in the 8th house and it is the version that does the most damage. It has never been tested, it does not follow from the positional rule, and it is the claim most often used to justify an expensive remedy.

Being manglik means I cannot marry a non-manglik.

The tradition treats a manglik and non-manglik pairing as needing more care, not as forbidden, and lists several cancellations that close the question entirely. Millions of such marriages exist.

Manglik dosha is rare and serious.

It is defined by Mars occupying six of twelve houses. Commonly quoted rates run from a quarter to a half of all charts depending on method. A category that broad cannot also be rare.

Every astrologer will give me the same manglik answer.

They will not, and this is well known within the practice. They count from different reference points and disagree about whether the 2nd house belongs on the list.

Kumbh vivah is a required fix.

It is a regional folk remedy with genuine history and no requirement attached. Its widespread commercial sale to people who were not told how common the dosha is deserves scepticism.

Doing a remedy removes the dosha from my chart.

The chart does not change. Mars is where it was at your birth minute and will be there for the rest of your life. What a remedy is traditionally said to change is the effect, which is a claim of a different kind entirely.

mistakes worth not making

  • accepting the label without asking the reference point. lagna, moon or Venus makes a real difference and it is a fair question.
  • skipping the cancellations. they are part of the classical procedure, not a loophole someone invented to be reassuring.
  • treating manglik as part of the 36 point ashtakoota score. it is not. it is a separate check run alongside.
  • paying for a remedy before understanding the placement. if the price arrived before the house number, that is the tell.
  • letting a chart carry a conversation that is really about something else. if the manglik question appeared only after another objection was raised, the chart is not the subject.

ask one question and the whole thing gets smaller: counted from where? lagna, moon or Venus. most people telling u ur manglik have never been asked, and the answer often decides whether u are manglik at all. a diagnosis that changes depending on where u stand is a category, not a condition.

the receipt

the house list, the cancellation conditions and the both-manglik rule are classical, drawn from the standard treatment of kuja dosha in Vedic literature and reflected in Brihat Parashara Hora Shastra's handling of Mars and the 7th house. the regional disagreement over the 2nd house is real and documented in practice rather than a claim we are making.

the base rate arithmetic is ours and it is simple: six houses of twelve is half the chart before any additional reference point is counted. the commonly quoted range of roughly a quarter to a half reflects different definitions in circulation, and we have given it as a range rather than a single number because a single number here would be false precision.

we know of no controlled study testing manglik status against marital outcomes. we are not aware of one because we do not believe one exists, and we would rather say that than imply evidence we cannot point to.

frequently asked questions

what is manglik dosha?

it is the label given when Mars sits in the 1st, 2nd, 4th, 7th, 8th or 12th house of a birth chart. it is also called mangal dosha, kuja dosha or chevvai dosham.

how do I know if I am manglik?

find which house Mars is in, counted from ur lagna. many astrologers also count from the moon and from Venus. the calculator on this page shows all three.

how common is manglik dosha?

very. six of twelve houses is half the chart, and commonly quoted rates run from about a quarter to a half depending on the definition used.

can two mangliks marry?

yes, and classical practice treats it as a cancellation of the dosha entirely. this is the tradition's own rule, and it tells u a lot about what the category actually is.

can a manglik marry a non-manglik?

yes. the tradition treats it as needing more care rather than being forbidden, and lists several cancellations that close the question. many such marriages exist.

what cancels manglik dosha?

commonly cited cancellations include Mars in its own sign or exaltation, Jupiter aspecting or occupying the 7th house, Mars conjunct Saturn or the nodes, and both partners being manglik.

is the 2nd house counted for manglik dosha?

it depends on the tradition. north Indian practice generally includes it. some southern practice does not. this single disagreement decides the answer for a lot of people.

which house is the worst for manglik dosha?

the 7th and 8th get the severe readings, the 7th because it is the marriage house and the 8th because of historical associations with longevity. many practitioners grade by house, though the grading is a judgment call rather than a fixed rule.

does manglik dosha affect anything other than marriage?

the classical claims are almost entirely about marriage and domestic life. broader claims about career or health are not standard.

is manglik dosha in the ashtakoota score?

no. ashtakoota scores 36 points across eight factors read from the two birth nakshatras. manglik status is a separate check run alongside it.

what is kumbh vivah?

a ceremonial marriage to a pot, tree or idol, said to absorb the dosha before a real marriage. it is a genuine regional folk practice and also one of the most commonly oversold remedies in the category.

does manglik dosha reduce with age?

some practitioners hold that its effect weakens after a certain age, often given as 28. this is a traditional claim rather than a calculated one, and the sources vary on the number.

is there evidence manglik dosha affects marriages?

no controlled study has tested it. it is a traditional interpretive category, and we would rather say that plainly than let the silence imply otherwise.

what should I do if I am told I am manglik?

ask what it was counted from, ask which cancellations were checked, and ask what happens if u do nothing. those three questions will tell u whether u are getting a reading or a sales pitch.

does a remedy remove manglik dosha from my chart?

the chart never changes. Mars stays where it was at ur birth minute. what remedies traditionally claim to change is the effect, which is a different kind of claim and one worth being clear-eyed about.

the five things worth remembering

  1. manglik dosha means Mars in one of six houses. that is the entire measurable content of the label.
  2. it applies to somewhere between a quarter and a half of all charts, which makes it a classification rather than a diagnosis.
  3. astrologers disagree because they count from different reference points and different house lists. always ask which.
  4. the both-manglik cancellation gives the game away: a defect that neutralises itself when doubled is a matching rule, not a curse.
  5. if a remedy price arrives before the house number does, that is the moment to stop and ask questions.
