finding a debilitated planet in ur chart feels like finding a typo in ur own name. it is ur chart, and something in it is at its weakest.

but vedic astrology does not stop at "debilitated". it lists specific conditions under which that weakness is cancelled, called neecha bhanga, and says a cancelled debilitation can even become a raj yoga, a royal combination. the tradition's favourite story is the planet that starts in the worst position and rises.

the short version

  • each planet is debilitated in one sign, opposite its exaltation.
  • neecha bhanga means the debilitation is cancelled by specific conditions.
  • key rule: certain planets connected to the debilitation sit in a kendra from the lagna or moon.
  • with several rules, cancellation is fairly common.
  • cancellation is not exaltation. texts describe rising after struggle.
  • results are timed by the dashas of the planets involved.

debilitation, briefly

A planet is debilitated in the sign directly opposite its sign of exaltation, where classical astrology says it has the most difficulty expressing its nature. Sun is debilitated in Libra, Moon in Scorpio, Mars in Cancer, Mercury in Pisces, Jupiter in Capricorn, Venus in Virgo and Saturn in Aries.

a useful image: a debilitated planet is a fish on land. it is still a fish, with all its abilities. the environment just works against it. the full explanation is in exalted and debilitated planets.

the cancellation rules

Classical texts cancel a planet's debilitation when: the lord of its debilitation sign is in a kendra from the ascendant or Moon; the lord of its exaltation sign is in a kendra; the planet exalted in its debilitation sign is in a kendra; it is aspected by its sign lord; or it exchanges signs with that lord.

rulein plain words
1. dispositor in a kendrathe planet that owns the sign of debilitation is in the 1st, 4th, 7th or 10th from the lagna or the moon
2. exaltation lord in a kendrathe planet that owns the debilitated planet's exaltation sign is in a kendra
3. the "opposite" planet in a kendrathe planet that is exalted in the debilitation sign is in a kendra
4. aspect by the dispositorthe owner of the debilitation sign aspects the planet
5. sign exchangethe debilitated planet and its dispositor sit in each other's signs
6. navamsa exaltationthe planet is exalted in the navamsa, a widely used later teaching

exact lists vary between texts and schools. phaladeepika is the clearest classical source, and most modern astrologers apply rules 1 to 5, with 6 as a supporting check.

tota says

never read "debilitated" and stop. ask three questions: where is its sign lord, where is its exaltation lord, and where is the planet that is exalted in that sign? if any of them sits in a kendra, the story changes.

worked examples

For Jupiter debilitated in Capricorn, the relevant planets are Saturn (lord of Capricorn), the Moon (lord of Cancer, Jupiter's exaltation sign) and Mars (exalted in Capricorn). If any of these is in a kendra from the ascendant or the Moon, Jupiter's debilitation is cancelled.

a concrete case. a capricorn lagna with jupiter in the 1st (capricorn, debilitated) and saturn in the 10th (libra). saturn, jupiter's dispositor, is in a kendra from the lagna. rule 1 is met. jupiter's debilitation is cancelled, and saturn, being exalted in libra, is strong enough to make that cancellation count.

note that the moon has no planet exalted in scorpio, and mercury is its own exaltation lord. these quirks are why each planet needs its own row.

when it becomes a raj yoga

Classical texts say a cancelled debilitation can produce results like a raj yoga, bringing status and success, often after early setbacks. The effect is considered strongest when the cancelling planets are themselves strong and well placed, and when the debilitated planet rules good houses for the ascendant.

the narrative the tradition attaches to it is memorable: a person who begins with a disadvantage in some area and, through effort, ends up stronger there than someone who started well. it is a story about resilience told through planets.

not every neecha bhanga is a raj yoga in practice. a debilitation cancelled by a weak, afflicted planet in the 8th house is technically cancelled and practically modest. strength still decides. compare with the standard raj yoga.

how to read a debilitated planet

Read a debilitated planet in layers: check for cancellation, then its navamsa strength, the houses it rules and occupies, its aspects, and when its dasha runs. A debilitated planet often describes an area of life that requires more conscious effort, not one that is doomed.

  1. check the cancellation rules. any one can change the picture.
  2. check the navamsa. stronger there means it improves.
  3. check what it rules. a debilitated lagna lord matters more than a debilitated 12th lord.
  4. check aspects. jupiter's aspect is traditionally protective.
  5. check timing. its dasha is when it speaks loudest.

check ur chart

Find any planet in its debilitation sign. Use the worked-example table to identify its dispositor, exaltation lord and the planet exalted there. Count from your ascendant and from your Moon to each of those planets. A 1, 4, 7 or 10 count meets the main cancellation rules.

about half of all charts have at least one debilitated planet, so this is a common exercise. the lagna and moon sign are the two starting points u need.

faq

what is neech bhanga raj yoga?

a combination where a debilitated planet's weakness is cancelled by specific conditions, and the planet is then said to give strong, often rising results. neecha means debilitated, bhanga means broken, and raj yoga means a royal combination.

what are the rules for neech bhanga?

the main ones: the lord of the debilitation sign in a kendra from the lagna or moon; the lord of the planet's exaltation sign in a kendra; the planet that is exalted in that sign in a kendra; the debilitated planet aspected by its sign lord; or an exchange between the planet and its sign lord.

which planets get debilitated where?

sun in libra, moon in scorpio, mars in cancer, mercury in pisces, jupiter in capricorn, venus in virgo, saturn in aries. each is debilitated in the sign opposite its exaltation.

is neech bhanga common?

fairly. there are several cancellation rules and each can be met from the lagna or the moon, so a good share of debilitated planets meet at least one. strength of the cancelling planets still matters.

is a debilitated planet always bad?

no. debilitation means the planet has difficulty expressing its significations easily. with cancellation, or good house placement and dasha timing, it can deliver well. many readers describe it as a planet that must work harder and so learns more.

does neech bhanga make a planet exalted?

no. it cancels the weakness rather than turning it into exaltation. classical texts say a cancelled debilitation can give results comparable to a raj yoga, often after early struggle.

what is neech bhanga for jupiter in capricorn?

jupiter's debilitation is cancelled if saturn, lord of capricorn, or the moon, lord of jupiter's exaltation sign cancer, or mars, which is exalted in capricorn, sits in a kendra from the lagna or the moon.

what is neech bhanga for sun in libra?

venus, lord of libra, or mars, lord of aries where the sun exalts, or saturn, exalted in libra, in a kendra from the lagna or moon cancels it. venus aspecting the sun also counts in many texts.

does navamsa matter for neech bhanga?

many astrologers check it. a planet debilitated in the d1 but exalted in the d9 navamsa is commonly read as recovering strength, and some count that as a form of cancellation.

when does neech bhanga raj yoga give results?

traditionally in the dasha or antardasha of the debilitated planet, or of the planets forming the cancellation. a common description is results arriving after initial difficulty.

can a debilitated planet be retrograde?

yes, mars, mercury, jupiter, venus and saturn can be. some traditions treat a retrograde debilitated planet as behaving more like an exalted one, though this is debated.

which classical texts describe neech bhanga?

phaladeepika by mantreswara gives clear rules, and brihat parashara hora shastra and later works discuss it. details vary slightly between texts.
