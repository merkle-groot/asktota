how accurate does ur birth time actually need to be

ur rising sign changes about every two hours, which is why no two births give quite the same chart. here is what a wrong birth time actually costs u, and what to do if u don't have an exact one.

AUGUST 5, 2026 · 7 MIN READ

"what time were u born" sounds like small talk. in a birth chart it is the single most powerful thing u type in.

but not every part of the chart cares about the clock the same amount. some barely shift in a whole day. one shifts so fast that twenty minutes can change the answer.

knowing which is which tells u how worried to be about that vague "somewhere around 6am" ur mum remembers.

three placements, three tolerances

the sun barely moves. about one degree a day. get ur birth date right and the sun lands in the correct spot no matter what time u type. the clock does not touch it.

the moon moves a bit. about thirteen degrees a day, so roughly one degree every two hours. being an hour out shifts it half a degree, which normally changes nothing. the exception is if u were born just as the moon was stepping into a new rashi. right at that edge, an hour matters. anywhere else it does not.

the rising sign is a whole different animal. it is not asking where a planet is. it is asking which slice of the zodiac was coming up over the eastern horizon at that exact minute.

the earth is spinning u through the sky the whole time, and it drags a full sign past the horizon every two hours. that works out to about one degree every four minutes.

so a birth certificate rounded to the nearest quarter hour is already three or four degrees off. that is often enough to move a planet into a different house, even when the rising sign itself stays put.

why the rising sign is this sensitive

the sun and moon are where they are. it hardly matters which country ur standing in when u look up at them.

the rising sign is not like that. it is about ur horizon, the line where ur sky meets ur ground. that line depends on exactly where u are and exactly what time it is, because the earth keeps turning u past it.

this is why twins born forty minutes apart can end up with different rising signs while sharing the same sun and moon sign. same day, same hospital, same parents. different minute, different horizon.

what actually counts as "accurate enough"

most hospital birth times in india are written down to the nearest five or fifteen minutes. that is fine for the sun and the moon, and fine for the rising sign too, unless u happen to sit right on an edge.

small rounding is not the real problem. the real problem is having no recorded time at all. "morning sometime", a relative's memory, or a number copied off a horoscope made decades ago by someone who was also guessing.

if that is u, do not fake it. a lagna built from a made-up time is not better than saying u do not know. read ur sun and moon instead. those two do not need the clock.

if u don't know your exact time

three things genuinely help, best first.

paperwork. a birth certificate or the hospital discharge summary. the time is often written on the discharge papers even when the certificate leaves it off.

an old chart. one an astrologer made years ago, back when someone in ur family still remembered the time properly.

rectification. this works backwards from big events in ur life to narrow down when u were probably born. it is a real technique with real limits. it gives u a window, not a magic exact minute.

and two things that do not help. rounding to the nearest hour because it feels close enough. or picking noon because it seems neutral. both hand u a confident, specific, wrong lagna, which is worse than admitting u are not sure.

the receipt

the sun's crawl of one degree a day, the moon's thirteen degrees a day, and the horizon's one degree every four minutes are plain astronomy. any astronomer would give u the same numbers. none of that is a vedic claim.

what is vedic is what gets built on top. the lagna sets ur entire house system, so it is the one place where being slightly off spreads through everything else.

faq

does my birth time affect my sun sign?

almost never. the sun moves about one degree a day, so unless u were born right on a sankranti, ur birth date on its own is enough.

how much does birth time affect my moon sign?

usually very little. the exception is if u were born just as the moon was changing rashi, which it does every two and a bit days. right on that edge, an hour can matter.

why is the rising sign so sensitive to birth time?

because it is set by which slice of the zodiac was coming up over the eastern horizon at that exact minute, and the earth's spin moves that point about one degree every four minutes.

what if I only have an approximate birth time?

ur sun and moon sign are still solid. treat the rising sign and the houses as rough, and go find a better time if u want to lean on those.

should I just guess a time to get a full chart?

no. a guessed time gives u a wrong lagna that looks completely confident. it is better to say the time is rough than to invent one.

the sun barely cares what time it was. the moon cares a little. the rising sign cares down to the minute. work out which one ur actually asking about before u panic about the clock.
