everyone knows navratri is nine nights. far fewer people can name the nine.

and almost nobody can say why it moved eleven days from last year, or why their cousin in bombay is fasting differently, or where that colour chart in the family group came from.

none of it is complicated. here is the whole structure, one night at a time, plus the parts that are older than u think and the parts that are much newer.

the short version

  • navratri is nine nights running from the first tithi of the bright fortnight, closing on dashami, which is dussehra.
  • each night belongs to one of the navadurga, nine forms, in a fixed order.
  • the dates move because it is set by tithi, not by the solar calendar. a lunar month is about 29.5 days.
  • there are actually four navratris a year. two are widely kept and two, the gupt navratris, are not.
  • it is occasionally eight days, because a tithi can vanish from the calendar entirely. that is arithmetic, not an omission.
  • the nine colour list is modern, popularised by newspapers. lovely, widely followed, and not classical.

the nine nights, one at a time

Each of the nine nights is devoted to one form of Durga, the Navadurga, in a fixed order. The sequence moves from the mountain-born daughter through the fierce forms and out into the giver of attainments, which is a narrative arc rather than a list.

nightformthe name meanswhat the night is about
1Shailaputridaughter of the mountainbeginnings, rootedness, the ground u stand on
2Brahmacharinione who practises austeritydiscipline, study, holding to a resolve
3Chandraghantabearer of the crescent bellcourage, and the calm that comes with it
4Kushmandathe cosmic eggcreation, warmth, the spark that starts things
5Skandamatamother of Skandanurture, protection, the fierceness of care
6Katyayanidaughter of sage Katyayanathe warrior form. resolve turned outward
7Kalaratrithe dark nightthe fiercest form. destruction of what has to go
8Mahagaurithe greatly luminousclarity after the dark night. relief, and cleanliness
9Siddhidatrigiver of attainmentscompletion. what the nine nights were for

read down the middle column and the shape is obvious: it starts grounded, builds discipline, turns fierce in the middle, breaks in the dark night, and resolves into clarity and completion.

then the tenth day, dashami, is dussehra or vijayadashami, and it is treated as one of the most auspicious days in the year for starting things. that placement is deliberate. u finish the nine, then u begin.

why the dates move every year

Navratri is fixed to a tithi, a lunar day, not to a solar date. A lunar month runs about 29.5 days and twelve of them fall roughly eleven days short of a solar year, so every lunar festival drifts backwards against the gregorian calendar until an extra month is inserted.

sharad navratri begins on pratipada, the first tithi of the bright fortnight of the month of ashwin, and runs to navami, the ninth.

that is a position of the moon relative to the sun, not a square on a calendar. so the gregorian date shifts by about eleven days each year, then jumps forward again when an adhik maas, an extra lunar month, is added to bring the two calendars back into line.

we took the whole lunisolar arrangement apart in why hindu festival dates move every year. it is the same machinery behind diwali, holi and every other date ur family argues about.

if u only remember one thing: the ninth night is kalaratri's clarity arriving, not the party ending. the sequence is designed so the hardest night comes seventh and the relief comes eighth. somebody thought about the order.

there are four navratris, not one

Four navratris fall in a year. Chaitra Navratri in spring and Sharad Navratri in autumn are the widely observed pair. Two more, in the months of Magha and Ashadha, are called Gupt Navratri, meaning hidden, and are kept far more quietly.

notice the spacing. four observances, roughly at the quarter points of the year, anchored to the lunar calendar. that is a structure, not a coincidence, and it is the kind of thing that gets lost when only the loudest one is visible.

why it is sometimes eight days

A tithi is not a day. It is the time the moon takes to gain twelve degrees on the sun, which can be shorter or longer than 24 hours. If a tithi begins and ends without ever being current at sunrise, it drops out of the calendar entirely, and the festival runs a day short.

this is called tithi kshaya, and it is one of the tidiest pieces of arithmetic in the whole calendar.

the moon's speed is not constant, so a tithi can run anywhere from about 19 to about 26 hours. most days contain a tithi change. occasionally an entire short tithi is swallowed between two sunrises.

when that happens inside navratri, two of the nine get combined onto a single day and the observance is eight days long. nothing has been skipped devotionally. the calendar simply had nowhere to put it.

the reverse also happens: a long tithi can be current at two consecutive sunrises, called tithi vriddhi, and the festival stretches. the panchang explainer covers how tithis are counted.

ghatasthapana and its muhurat

Ghatasthapana is the establishing of a pot, usually with barley sown in earth around it, on the first day. It formally opens the observance and it carries its own muhurat, traditionally in the first third of the day.

this is where navratri touches the timing tradition properly. the day is fixed by the tithi and nobody chooses it, but the window inside the day is chosen, and that is a muhurta problem.

the usual guidance is the first third of the daylight period, while pratipada is still current, avoiding the periods traditionally set aside such as rahu kaal. where that is not possible, abhijit around local noon is the common fallback.

the barley matters too, and it is a nice detail. the sprouting over nine days is a visible measure of the observance, which is a very old way of making an abstract commitment into something u can look at. what a muhurat is goes through how a window like this gets selected.

the colour chart, honestly

The nine-colour list is a modern popular convention. It spread through newspapers in western India over recent decades and is now genuinely widespread. It is not prescribed in classical sources, and the specific colours vary between the lists that circulate.

this is worth saying plainly, and it is worth saying warmly, because both halves are true.

it is not classical. u will not find a nine colour sequence in the older devotional texts. the colours also differ between published lists, which is exactly what u would expect from something that grew through media rather than through liturgy.

and it is real now. millions of people coordinate what they wear across nine days because of it. that is a living tradition forming in front of us, and there is nothing lesser about a custom that is forty years old instead of four hundred.

the only thing worth resisting is the framing that it is ancient. say it is a lovely modern convention and it loses none of its charm.

the older colour association that is textual runs the other way: each form of durga has iconography, and the descriptions carry colour in them. that is a different thing from a nine day dress code.

why ur cousin is fasting differently

Navratri practice is regional and always has been. What is eaten, how many days are kept, whether the observance concentrates on the first nights or the last, and which narrative is emphasised, all differ by community.

  • bengal concentrates on the last days as durga puja: the idol installed on shashthi, worshipped through ashtami and navami, immersed on dashami.
  • gujarat keeps all nine nights with garba and dandiya, which is why the colour convention took root there first.
  • north india commonly keeps ashtami or navami as the day for kanya pujan, and many families fast on the first and eighth or ninth days rather than all nine.
  • the south observes golu, arranging dolls on tiered steps, with the last three days divided between durga, lakshmi and saraswati.

and the fasting rules themselves are household-level. grains avoided or not, salt permitted or only sendha namak, one meal or none. there is no single correct version, and anyone telling u otherwise is describing their own family rather than the tradition.

faq

what are the nine forms of durga?

shailaputri, brahmacharini, chandraghanta, kushmanda, skandamata, katyayani, kalaratri, mahagauri and siddhidatri. together they are the navadurga, and the order is fixed.

why do navratri dates change every year?

because it is set by tithi, not by the solar calendar. sharad navratri begins on the first tithi of the bright fortnight of ashwin, and a lunar month is about 29.5 days, so it drifts against the gregorian calendar.

how many navratris are there in a year?

four. chaitra navratri in spring and sharad navratri in autumn are the widely observed ones. two more, in magha and ashadha, are called gupt navratri and are kept much more quietly.

why is navratri sometimes eight days?

tithi kshaya. a tithi can begin and end without ever being current at sunrise, so it drops out of the calendar and the festival runs a day short. it is a calendar artefact, not an omission.

what is ghatasthapana?

the establishing of the pot on the first day, which formally opens the observance. it has its own muhurat, usually in the first third of the day, and it is avoided during specific periods.

are the navratri colours traditional?

the nine colour list is a modern convention, popularised through newspapers in western india over the last few decades. it is genuinely enjoyable and it is not classical, and both of those things can be true.

what is the difference between navratri and durga puja?

they are the same season observed differently. durga puja in bengal concentrates on the last few days, with the idol installed on shashthi and immersed on dashami.

what happens on the tenth day?

dashami, which is dussehra or vijayadashami. it closes the nine nights and is treated as one of the most auspicious days of the year for beginnings.

does navratri have anything to do with the grahas?

not directly. the timing is lunar and tithi-based, so it is calendar astronomy rather than a planetary reading. the observance itself is devotional, not astrological.

why does the fasting differ between families?

because the rules are regional and always have been. what counts as permitted food, whether grains are avoided, and how many days are kept vary by community and by household.

what is the story behind the nine nights?

the widely told version is the battle between durga and mahishasura, concluding in victory on the tenth day. the specifics vary by region, and several traditions attach different narratives to the same nine nights.

which navratri is the biggest?

sharad navratri, in the autumn. it leads into dussehra and then into diwali about twenty days later, which makes it the opening of the busiest stretch of the indian festival calendar.
