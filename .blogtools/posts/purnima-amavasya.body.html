half of the big hindu festivals sit on one of two days: the full moon or the new moon. there is a reason.

purnima is the full moon and amavasya is the new moon. in the vedic calendar they are not just phases but tithis, lunar days defined by exactly how far the moon is from the sun. they mark the two ends of every lunar month. eclipses can only happen on them. and each carries its own traditional character.

the short version

  • a tithi is the moon gaining 12 degrees on the sun. 30 tithis make a lunar month.
  • purnima: moon 180° from the sun, full. end of the waxing fortnight.
  • amavasya: moon with the sun, dark. end of the waning fortnight.
  • solar eclipses only on amavasya, lunar eclipses only on purnima.
  • purnima is linked to fullness and celebration; amavasya to ancestors and release.
  • diwali is on an amavasya. holi and raksha bandhan are on purnimas.

what a tithi is

A tithi is a lunar day: the time the Moon takes to move 12 degrees further from the Sun. Thirty tithis make a lunar month. Because the Moon's speed varies, tithis range from about 19 to 26 hours, so they do not line up with solar days. Purnima and Amavasya are the 15th and 30th tithis.

the moon laps the sun once every 29.5 days or so. split that 360 degree lap into 30 equal steps of 12 degrees and each step is a tithi. the first 15, as the moon pulls away from the sun and grows brighter, are shukla paksha, the bright fortnight. the next 15, as it closes in again, are krishna paksha, the dark fortnight.

the tithi is one of the five limbs of the panchang, and the one that decides most festival dates.

purnima, the full moon

Purnima is the 15th tithi of the bright fortnight, ending when the Moon is exactly opposite the Sun and fully lit. It is traditionally associated with fullness, completion, devotion and celebration, and is a common day for vows, fasts, worship and festivals.

purnima is the moon at its strongest in the vedic sense: the moon is the mind, and a full moon is the mind at full brightness. many vows, like satyanarayan puja, are traditionally kept on purnima.

each lunar month is often named after the nakshatra the moon occupies on its purnima. kartik, for example, comes from krittika, chaitra from chitra, and shravana from the nakshatra of the same name.

amavasya, the new moon

Amavasya is the last tithi of the dark fortnight, when the Moon and Sun are together and the Moon is invisible. It is traditionally the day of the ancestors, used for tarpan and shraddha, charity and spiritual practice, and generally avoided for beginning marriages or new ventures.

the name means roughly "dwelling together": the moon and sun share the same part of the sky. because the moon, the mind, is dark, amavasya is treated as an inward day. it is the natural day for remembering ancestors, which is why pitra dosha remedies and tarpan cluster here.

but "avoided for beginnings" is not "unlucky". diwali, one of the most celebrated nights of the year, is on an amavasya, lit with lamps precisely because the night is darkest.

why eclipses only land on these days

A solar eclipse requires the Moon to pass between Earth and Sun, which can only happen at new moon, Amavasya. A lunar eclipse requires Earth between Sun and Moon, which can only happen at full moon, Purnima. Both also require the Moon to be near one of its nodes, Rahu or Ketu, which is why eclipses do not happen every month.

the moon's orbit is tilted about five degrees to the sun's path. most months the new moon passes a little above or below the sun, and the full moon slips past earth's shadow. only when the alignment happens near rahu or ketu does the shadow land. that geometry is the real astronomy behind the myth of rahu swallowing the sun. more in eclipses and sutak.

the festivals each one carries

Major Purnima festivals include Guru Purnima, Raksha Bandhan, Sharad Purnima, Kartik Purnima, Holi and Buddha Purnima. Major Amavasya observances include Diwali, Mahalaya Amavasya at the end of Pitru Paksha, Mauni Amavasya, and Somvati Amavasya when the new moon falls on a Monday.

the moon phase in ur birth chart

Your birth tithi shows the Moon's phase when you were born. Classical astrology treats a bright Moon, waxing and well away from the Sun, as stronger, and a Moon close to Amavasya as weaker in paksha bala. A full Moon birth is often read as emotionally expressive; a new Moon birth as more inward.

this is part of why astrologers say the moon's strength depends on its distance from the sun, not just its sign. a moon in its exaltation sign, taurus, but one day from amavasya, is a dimmer moon than its sign suggests. ur moon sign is half the story; ur birth tithi is the other half.

faq

what is purnima?

the full moon tithi, the 15th and last tithi of shukla paksha, the waxing fortnight. it is when the moon is 180 degrees from the sun and fully lit.

what is amavasya?

the new moon tithi, the last tithi of krishna paksha, the waning fortnight. the moon and sun are together in the sky, so the moon is dark.

what is a tithi?

a lunar day, the time the moon takes to gain 12 degrees on the sun. there are 30 tithis in a lunar month, 15 waxing and 15 waning. because the moon's speed varies, a tithi lasts roughly 19 to 26 hours.

what is the difference between purnima and amavasya?

purnima is the full moon, with the moon opposite the sun; amavasya is the new moon, with the moon conjunct the sun. purnima is traditionally linked with fullness and celebration, amavasya with ancestors, introspection and endings.

why do eclipses only happen on purnima or amavasya?

a solar eclipse needs the moon between earth and sun, which only happens at new moon. a lunar eclipse needs earth between sun and moon, which only happens at full moon. both also need the moon near rahu or ketu.

is amavasya inauspicious?

it is traditionally avoided for starting new ventures, marriages and auspicious ceremonies, but it is highly auspicious for ancestor rites, spiritual practice and some festivals, including diwali.

is purnima good for starting new things?

purnima is widely considered auspicious and is used for vows, fasts, worship and many festivals. for specific undertakings astrologers still check the full muhurat, not the tithi alone.

which festivals fall on purnima?

guru purnima, raksha bandhan, sharad purnima, kartik purnima, holi on phalguna purnima and buddha purnima, among many others.

which festivals fall on amavasya?

diwali, mahalaya amavasya at the end of pitru paksha, mauni amavasya and somvati amavasya, when a new moon falls on a monday.

does the full moon affect behaviour?

it is a popular belief, but large studies of hospital admissions, crime and emergency calls have generally not found reliable full moon effects on behaviour.

why do some calendars count the month from purnima?

india uses two systems. amanta months end on amavasya, common in the south and west. purnimanta months end on purnima, common in the north. the festivals fall on the same days; only the month names shift.

what should i do on amavasya?

traditional observances include tarpan for ancestors, charity, quiet reflection, and in some traditions fasting. it is treated as a day for completing and releasing rather than beginning.
