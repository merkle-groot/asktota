ur birth nakshatra already narrows the sky to one twenty-seventh. the pada narrows it again, to one hundred and eighth.

a nakshatra pada is a quarter of a nakshatra: 3 degrees and 20 minutes of sky. it decides which navamsa sign ur moon falls in, gives the traditional first syllable for a name, and quietly explains why the navamsa chart and the nakshatras fit together so neatly.

it is a small division that does a lot of work.

the short version

  • each nakshatra is 13°20′ wide and splits into four padas of 3°20′.
  • 27 nakshatras × 4 padas = 108 padas.
  • 12 signs × 9 navamsas is also 108. each pada is one navamsa.
  • so ur moon's pada gives ur moon's navamsa sign directly.
  • each pada has a traditional name syllable, used in namakarana.
  • some padas get special attention: vargottama (strong) and gandanta (sensitive).

what a pada is

A nakshatra pada is one quarter of a nakshatra, spanning 3 degrees 20 minutes of the zodiac. There are 108 padas across the 27 nakshatras. Your birth pada is the quarter of your Moon's nakshatra that the Moon occupied at birth, and it refines the nakshatra's meaning with the sign of its navamsa.

think of a nakshatra as a neighbourhood and the padas as its four streets. people on every street share the neighbourhood's character. the street adds its own flavour.

the moon crosses one nakshatra in about a day, so it crosses one pada in roughly six hours. that is why u need a birth time to know ur pada, even if u can guess ur nakshatra from the date.

why 108 is not a coincidence

The zodiac has two grids: 12 signs of 30 degrees and 27 nakshatras of 13 degrees 20 minutes. They only line up every 40 degrees, which is three nakshatras or one and a third signs. Dividing both into 108 parts of 3 degrees 20 minutes makes them mesh perfectly, and each part is both a pada and a navamsa.

each sign holds exactly nine padas, which is why the ninth-divisional chart is called the navamsa, "ninth part". the navamsa signs run aries, taurus, gemini and onwards from the very first pada of ashwini, round the whole zodiac nine times.

the pattern repeats every three nakshatras. so if u know ur nakshatra's position in that cycle of three and ur pada, u know the navamsa sign without a calculator.

108 also turns up all over indian tradition, from mala beads to temple steps. the astronomical reason here is simple arithmetic: it is the smallest number that both 12 and 27 divide into evenly when each sign holds a whole number of nakshatra quarters.

what each pada adds to a reading

The pada adds the qualities of its navamsa sign and that sign's ruling planet to the nakshatra's own meaning. A common teaching also maps padas to the four purusharthas by element: fire padas to dharma, earth to artha, air to kama and water to moksha. Together these give each of the 108 its own shade.

so two people with a moon in ashwini share the nakshatra's quick, pioneering character. the one in pada 1 (aries navamsa, fire) is read as more about drive and principle. the one in pada 4 (cancer navamsa, water) as more inward and emotional. same star, different streets.

this is a teaching device, not a hard rule. in an actual reading, the pada's navamsa sign is weighed with the planet's dignity in the divisional charts and everything else in the chart.

padas and name syllables

Each of the 108 padas carries a traditional syllable. In the naming ceremony, namakarana, many families start a child's name with the syllable of the Moon's birth pada. Ashwini's four padas, for example, are Chu, Che, Cho and La.

vargottama and gandanta

A planet is vargottama when it occupies the same sign in the birth chart and the navamsa, which happens in specific padas: the first navamsa of movable signs, the fifth of fixed signs and the ninth of dual signs. Gandanta padas sit at the junctions where water signs end and fire signs begin.

vargottama is read as strengthening, because the planet's sign is confirmed at two levels. examples: the first pada of ashwini (aries in aries), the second pada of rohini (taurus in taurus).

gandanta means a knot. three places in the zodiac have both a sign boundary and a nakshatra boundary landing at once, where a water sign hands over to a fire sign:

how to find ur pada

Find your Moon's exact sidereal longitude from a birth chart. Work out how far it has travelled into its nakshatra, then divide by 3 degrees 20 minutes and round up. Zero to 3°20′ is pada 1, up to 6°40′ pada 2, up to 10°00′ pada 3, and up to 13°20′ pada 4.

worked example: a moon at 15° taurus. rohini runs from 10°00′ to 23°20′ taurus, so the moon is 5°00′ into rohini. that is past 3°20′ but before 6°40′, so rohini pada 2, taurus navamsa, vargottama for the moon.

in practice any vedic app prints it. if u know ur moon sign but not ur pada, the missing piece is almost always the birth time.

myths, one at a time

You can know your pada from your birth date alone.

The Moon crosses a pada in about six hours. Without a birth time you can only narrow it down.

Your name must start with your pada syllable.

It is a traditional naming practice, not a rule with consequences. Many families adapt it or skip it.

Gandanta padas are unlucky.

They are read as sensitive junctions that deserve careful interpretation. Nothing in the texts makes a birth there a sentence.

Padas are a separate system from the navamsa.

They are the same 108 divisions seen from two directions: as quarters of nakshatras and as ninths of signs.

faq

what is a nakshatra pada?

a quarter of a nakshatra. each nakshatra is 13 degrees 20 minutes wide and splits into four padas of 3 degrees 20 minutes each. ur birth pada is the quarter the moon was in when u were born.

how many padas are there?

108. there are 27 nakshatras and each has 4 padas. 108 is also 12 signs times 9 navamsas, which is why padas and the navamsa chart line up exactly.

what is the difference between pada and charan?

nothing. charan, or charana, is another word for pada. both mean foot or quarter.

how do i find my nakshatra pada?

u need ur moon's exact degree, which needs ur birth date, time and place. divide the moon's position within its nakshatra by 3 degrees 20 minutes. any vedic chart tool will print the pada next to the nakshatra.

how are padas related to the navamsa chart?

each pada is exactly one navamsa. the first pada of ashwini is the aries navamsa, the second is taurus, and so on through the zodiac nine times. so ur moon's pada tells u ur moon's navamsa sign directly.

how do padas decide baby names?

each of the 108 padas has a traditional syllable. ashwini's four padas are chu, che, cho and la, for example. the naming tradition, namakarana, starts the child's name with the syllable of the moon's birth pada.

which pada is best?

none is best in general. some are given special notice: vargottama padas, where a planet's sign and navamsa match, are read as strong, and gandanta padas at water to fire sign junctions are read as sensitive.

what is vargottama?

a planet in the same sign in the rashi chart and the navamsa. it happens in the first pada of a movable sign, the middle navamsa of a fixed sign, and the last of a dual sign. it is traditionally read as strengthening the planet.

what does each pada number mean?

a common reading links each pada to a purushartha by the element of its navamsa sign: fire with dharma, earth with artha, air with kama and water with moksha. in ashwini that gives pada 1 dharma, 2 artha, 3 kama, 4 moksha.

what is gandanta?

the knot where a water sign ends and a fire sign begins: the last pada of ashlesha, jyeshtha and revati and the first pada of magha, mula and ashwini. traditional astrology treats a moon or lagna there as needing extra care in reading.

does my pada change my dasha?

not the mahadasha lord, which comes from the nakshatra. but the exact position inside the nakshatra, which the pada roughly shows, sets how much of the first dasha was left at birth.

do padas matter for kundli matching?

the ashtakoota system works mainly from the nakshatra and moon sign. padas come in for specific checks, such as some traditions softening nadi dosha when the two moons are in the same nakshatra but different padas.
